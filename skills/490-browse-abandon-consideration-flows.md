# Browse-abandon + consideration flows (the pre-cart Tier-1 pillar)

> The browse-abandon flow catches the **80–95% of site visitors who browse-only and never reach the cart** — by far the largest pool of pre-purchase intent on any DTC site. Klaviyo's 2024 benchmark report puts browse-abandon conversion at **0.6–1.5%** (vs cart-abandon's 5–15%), but absolute volume is **5–10× larger** because the browse pool is 5–10× the cart pool, so total revenue from browse-abandon often matches or exceeds cart-abandon. The same logic extends to **category-view + search-no-click + search-no-conversion** (the 3 consideration-event siblings) — each catches a different pre-cart intent signal at a different price-sensitivity / engagement tier. Without browse-abandon + consideration flows, the operator captures only the **5–20%** of site visitors who reach the cart; with them, the operator captures the **30–50%** who show *any* signal of purchase intent. Ship after Move #1 (cart-abandon), Move #4 (welcome), and Move #7 (SMS welcome + cart-abandon) are all live and producing baseline data — the consideration flows reuse the same Klaviyo + Postscript + Smile wiring and the same segmentation hygiene, so they ship in ~4 hours of operator time and return a Year-1 ROI band of **8:1–28:1** at the default $500k–$10M GMV DTC brand.

## When to use this skill

You have:

- Move #1 (cart-abandon flow) shipped with ≥6-week revenue baseline
- Move #4 (welcome series) shipped with ≥6-week revenue baseline
- Move #7 (SMS welcome + cart-abandon) shipped with ≥4-week revenue baseline
- Klaviyo onsite JS installed and `Viewed Product` + `Searched Site` + `Added to Cart` + `Placed Order` events flowing
- Postscript (or Attentive) SMS wired with sender registration + opt-in compliance (TCPA/GDPR)
- Klaviyo + Postscript Flow Library pricing tier (typically Klaviyo's Email + SMS Marketing plan or higher)
- ≥$2000/mo paid-media spend (the brand has a steady inflow of new visitors to "browse")
- Operator capacity: 4-hour kickoff block + 1-hour weekly maintenance block for the first 8 weeks

You do NOT have:

- A browse-abandon flow that catches the 80–95% who browse-only and never add-to-cart
- A category-view flow that catches visitors who browse a category but don't view a product
- A search-no-click or search-no-conversion flow that catches visitors who search but don't engage
- An add-to-cart-no-checkout flow that catches visitors who add to cart but abandon before checkout-start
- A/SMS, so you're leaving the bulk of pre-purchase intent on the table
- Per-product browse-abandon with dynamic product blocks (Klaviyo's `Viewed Product` event feeds the dynamic block; without the dynamic block, the email is generic and converts 30–50% lower)
- Discount-stacking guards (browse-abandon 10% off + cart-abandon 10% off + winback 15% off + sunset 20% off all firing on the same customer = margin destruction or discount-trained-customer)
- A/Smart-Sending guard on browse-abandon so a single visitor doesn't get 4 emails + 2 SMS in 1 day

## The build (time estimate: ~4 hours)

### Phase 1 — Klaviyo + Postscript + Smile flow wiring (1.5h)

1. **Create browse-abandon flow in Klaviyo**: Flows → Create Flow → "Browse Abandonment" → Trigger: `Viewed Product` → filter: `Has not placed order in last 7 days` AND `Has not added to cart in last 4 hours` AND `Has not redeemed discount in last 30 days`. Use a Time Delay (4 hours) before the first email, then a Conditional Split that branches on `Has not added to cart in last 4 hours` (so the flow exits when a customer adds to cart and the cart-abandon flow takes over).
2. **Add 3 email steps + 2 SMS steps**:
   - Email 1 at 4h: subject "Still thinking about [Product]?" — dynamic product block = last viewed product + 2 recommendations (Klaviyo's "Viewed Product" event feeds the dynamic block; without it, the email is generic and converts 30–50% lower). No discount in email 1 (test discount-in-email-1 vs no-discount for first 30 days, then keep the winner).
   - Email 2 at 24h: subject "[Product] is back in stock" or "Saw this yesterday?" — dynamic product block = same product + a different recommendation angle (e.g. "Picked for you based on your browsing"). No discount.
   - Email 3 at 72h: subject "Last chance — 10% off [Product]" — dynamic product block + 10% off coupon (`KLAVIYO_BROWSE10` or per-flow unique code). **This is the only email in the flow that carries a discount** — the first two are pure social-proof + product-detail angles.
   - SMS 1 at 24h: "Saw you looking at [Product] on [Brand] — still thinking about it? [link]" — use Postscript's two-way SMS so customers can reply STOP / questions / etc.
   - SMS 2 at 72h: "10% off [Product] for the next 24 hours — [link]" — only fires if customer has NOT redeemed a discount in last 30 days.
3. **Set Smart Sending window to 16 hours** (Klaviyo default is 16h; verify in Flow Settings). This prevents a single customer from getting 4 emails + 2 SMS in 1 day if they also get the cart-abandon flow + welcome flow.
4. **Add the 4 sibling flows in the same library**:
   - Category-view flow (trigger: `Viewed Category`; 1 email + 1 SMS at 2h; no discount; dynamic category block).
   - Search-no-click flow (trigger: `Searched Site` AND no `Viewed Product` in 1h; 1 email at 1h; no discount; show "Popular products in [search term]" block).
   - Search-no-conversion flow (trigger: `Searched Site` + `Viewed Product` + no `Added to Cart` in 24h; 1 email at 24h + 1 at 72h; 1 SMS at 48h for high-AOV segment; no discount).
   - Add-to-cart-no-checkout flow (trigger: `Added to Cart` AND no `Checkout Started` in 2h; 1 email at 2h + 1 SMS at 2h; no discount — let the cart-abandon flow handle the 4h+ window).

### Phase 2 — Discount-stacking guard + segmentation hygiene (1h)

5. **Wire the discount-stacking guard** in every browse-abandon + consideration flow: add a flow filter `Has not redeemed a discount in last 30 days` (Klaviyo Segment → "Discount Redeemed (Last 30d)" → use as suppression). This is the canonical anti-discount-stacking guard from Move #1's playbook; without it, a single customer can stack browse-abandon 10% off + cart-abandon 10% off + winback 15% off + sunset 20% off in a single 7-day window, training the customer to wait for the next discount and destroying margin.
6. **Create 3 baseline segments** if not already shipped (these were Move #1's prerequisites but worth re-asserting for browse-abandon):
   - `active_customer`: ≥1 order in last 90d
   - `lapsed_customer`: no order in 90–365d
   - `subscriber_not_buyer`: subscribed but 0 orders
7. **Add 3 high-intent segmentation overlays** specific to browse-abandon:
   - `viewed_product_high_value`: viewed product in last 7d AND last 30d AOV > $100 → exclude from 10% off browse-abandon (they'll buy at full price with a 1.5% CVR, no need to subsidize)
   - `viewed_product_bfcm_active`: viewed product between Nov 15 – Dec 24 → route to BFCM variant (15% off instead of 10% off, longer cadence)
   - `viewed_product_low_stock`: viewed product in last 7d AND product has ≤10 units in stock → add urgency email ("Only 4 left — others are looking") at 48h
8. **Set the discount-floor rule**: **1-discount-per-customer-per-30-days**. Track in a `discount_redeemed` custom property on the Klaviyo profile; update via Klaviyo webhook on `Order Placed` event with `coupon` set. All flows (cart-abandon, browse-abandon, winback, sunset, birthday) consult this property via a Segment filter.

### Phase 3 — A/B testing + per-cohort optimization (1h)

9. **A/B test the first email's subject line** for 30 days: 2 variants — A) "Still thinking about [Product]?" (curiosity) vs B) "[Product] is back at [Brand]" (availability). Use Klaviyo's built-in A/B test framework. After 30 days, lock the winning subject and run the next test (send time).
10. **A/B test send time** for the next 30 days: 2 variants — A) email at 4h post-view, B) email at 6h post-view. Lock the winner, then test day-of-week for the next 30 days.
11. **Per-cohort send-time optimization**: Klaviyo's "Send at the customer's local time-of-day" feature (Send Time Optimization, requires Klaviyo's Email + SMS Marketing plan or higher). For brands with >30% international traffic, this lifts browse-abandon open rate 5–12% (per Klaviyo 2024 benchmarks).
12. **A/B test the discount-in-email-1 vs no-discount** for the first 30 days. The canonical finding (per Klaviyo 2024 + Omnisend 2024 + Sailor Royale 2024 benchmarks) is that the 72h email is the only one that benefits from a discount; email 1 and email 2 convert equally well without one, and removing the discount preserves 5–8% margin per order.

### Phase 4 — Verification + monitoring (30 min)

13. **Verify each flow is published + live**: Klaviyo Analytics → Flows → confirm `browse_abandon_v1`, `category_view_v1`, `search_no_click_v1`, `search_no_conversion_v1`, `add_to_cart_no_checkout_v1` all show "Live" status with >0 sends in last 7 days. For Postscript, confirm `browse_abandon_sms_v1` is live with >0 sends in last 7 days.
14. **Set up the 5 canonical KPIs** in your dashboard (per the "Verification" section below): browse-abandon revenue per 1k `Viewed Product` events, browse-abandon email CVR, browse-abandon SMS CVR, category-view CVR, search-no-conversion CVR.
15. **Run the 4-week performance review** (every Monday for 4 weeks): for each flow, check (a) open rate ≥20%, (b) CTR ≥2%, (c) CVR ≥0.6%, (d) revenue per 1k events ≥$300, (e) unsubscribe rate <0.5%. If any KPI is below floor for 2 consecutive weeks, swap the email template, subject line, or send time.

## Common pitfalls (15 from real builds)

1. **Treating browse-abandon as "cart-abandon lite" with the same 4h cadence.** Cart-abandon converts at 5–15% because the customer has already raised their hand (added to cart). Browse-abandon converts at 0.6–1.5% because the customer is still evaluating. The cadence is different: cart-abandon fires at 1h + 4h + 24h (compressed, urgent); browse-abandon fires at 4h + 24h + 72h (spaced, social-proof-led). Using cart-abandon's cadence on browse-abandon produces a 30–50% lower CVR.
2. **Skipping the dynamic product block.** The single biggest CVR-killer in browse-abandon is a static email ("Here are some products you might like") instead of a dynamic block showing the *exact* product the customer viewed + 2 recommendations. Klaviyo's `Viewed Product` event feeds the dynamic block; using a static block converts 30–50% lower.
3. **Putting the 10% off discount in email 1.** Discount-in-email-1 trains the customer to wait for a discount on every email, devalues the brand, and reduces total browse-abandon revenue by 8–15% per Klaviyo 2024 benchmarks. The 72h email is the only one that should carry a discount; email 1 and email 2 are pure social-proof + product-detail.
4. **Firing the SMS at the same time as the email.** A common anti-pattern is to send the SMS and email at the same hour (e.g. both at 24h). The customer gets hit with two messages in their inbox + phone, the unsubscribe rate doubles, and CVR doesn't lift 2×. The correct pattern: stagger the SMS by 4–8h from the email (email at 24h, SMS at 28–32h), so each channel is a separate touchpoint.
5. **Forgetting Smart Sending.** Without the Smart Sending window (Klaviyo default 16h), a single customer can get 4 emails + 2 SMS in 1 day (browse-abandon email 1 + browse-abandon SMS 1 + cart-abandon email 1 + welcome email 2 + birthday email + winback SMS). The unsubscribe rate spikes 3–5× and the customer's "received" rate per email drops 20–30%.
6. **No discount-stacking guard.** A common anti-pattern is to ship browse-abandon + cart-abandon + winback + sunset + birthday flows all with discounts, and let a single customer stack 3–4 discounts in a 30-day window. Margin destruction: 5–15% of revenue gets discounted twice, the customer learns to wait for discounts. **The fix: 1-discount-per-customer-per-30-days rule** via the `discount_redeemed` custom property + Klaviyo Segment filter on every flow.
7. **Firing browse-abandon to existing customers.** A common anti-pattern is to send browse-abandon to customers who already purchased the product in the last 30 days. Filter: `Has not placed order in last 7 days` on the browse-abandon flow. Without this filter, 20–40% of browse-abandon recipients have already bought the product and the email is noise → unsubscribe.
8. **Firing category-view to all site visitors.** The category-view flow is the most expensive-to-send flow per Klaviyo's pricing model (each email costs ~$0.001–$0.005 depending on plan). Sending it to every visitor blows the Klaviyo email budget. Filter: `Viewed Category` + `Has not placed order in last 30 days` + `Has not viewed category in last 24h` (so repeat category-browsers don't get spammed). Cost reduction: 40–60% without CVR impact.
9. **Using "Searched Site" without saving the search term.** The `Searched Site` event has a `Search Term` property that should be used in the email body ("Popular products in [search term]"). Without it, the email is generic and the customer doesn't recognize their own search — CVR drops 40–60% on the search-no-click flow.
10. **Firing the add-to-cart-no-checkout flow at the same time as the cart-abandon flow.** Both flows trigger on `Added to Cart`; without coordination, a customer can get the add-to-cart-no-checkout email + SMS at 2h AND the cart-abandon email + SMS at 4h — 4 messages in 2 hours. The fix: add a flow filter on the add-to-cart-no-checkout flow `Has not started checkout in last 4 hours` AND on the cart-abandon flow `Has not started checkout in last 4 hours` + the flow exit condition `Has started checkout` so the flows don't overlap.
11. **Using a single discount code across the flow.** A common anti-pattern is to put `KLAVIYO_BROWSE10` in every email in the flow. The customer receives the same code 3 times in 3 days → uses it on the 4th day when they would have bought at full price → 10% margin gone. The fix: use per-flow unique codes (`KLAVIYO_BROWSE10_3`, `KLAVIYO_BROWSE10_4`) so each email carries a different code, trackable per-email redemption.
12. **Not segmenting by high-value vs low-value shoppers.** A high-LTV customer who has 5 orders in the last 12 months doesn't need a 10% off discount to convert on a $200 product. The browse-abandon flow should have a `viewed_product_high_value` filter (per Phase 2 step 7) that excludes customers with last-30d AOV > $100 from the 10% off email. Margin preservation: 5–12% per Klaviyo 2024 benchmarks.
13. **Sending a discount the customer already used.** A common anti-pattern is to send the 10% off email in the browse-abandon flow to a customer who redeemed a 10% off code in the last 7 days. The customer is annoyed ("I already used this code, why are you sending it again?") and unsubscribes. The fix: track `discount_redeemed` custom property with the timestamp of last redemption; flow filter `Last discount redeemed > 30 days ago` OR `Last discount redeemed is empty`.
14. **Firing the browse-abandon flow from the wrong page (e.g. homepage).** Klaviyo's `Viewed Product` event fires from PDP only; `Viewed Category` from category pages; `Viewed Page` from any page. A common anti-pattern is to use `Viewed Page` as the browse-abandon trigger — the customer is then emailed for visiting the About page, the FAQ, the Contact page. The fix: trigger on `Viewed Product` for browse-abandon, `Viewed Category` for category-view, never `Viewed Page` for either.
15. **Failing to A/B test subject lines + send times.** A common anti-pattern is to ship the flow with the default subject + default send time and never A/B test. Conversion lands at the 25th percentile of Klaviyo benchmarks. The fix: 30-day A/B tests on first-email subject line → 30-day A/B tests on send time → 30-day A/B tests on day-of-week. Lift: 15–35% just from subject + send-time optimization (Klaviyo 2024 + Omnisend 2024 + Sailor Royale 2024).

## How to extend this skill

1. **Per-cohort browse-abandon segmentation**: instead of one browse-abandon flow, create 3 variants — high-AOV customer browse-abandon (no discount, premium products), mid-AOV customer browse-abandon (10% off at 72h, full catalog), low-AOV / first-time-visitor browse-abandon (15% off at 48h, top 20 bestsellers). Expected lift: 20–40% on browse-abandon revenue.
2. **Personalized subject lines via Movable Ink + Cordial + Liveclicker**: instead of `[Product] is back in stock`, use a dynamic subject that pulls the product image into the subject preview (Gmail's "AMP for Email" + Movable Ink's DaVinci). Expected lift: 8–15% on open rate.
3. **Cross-device browse-abandon stitch**: a customer views a product on mobile at 9am, opens the email on desktop at 11am, clicks the email CTA on mobile at 1pm. Without cross-device stitch, the email CTA is generic ("Here are some products you might like"). With cross-device stitch (Klaviyo + Segment + Rudderstack), the CTA links to the *same* product the customer viewed on mobile. Expected lift: 15–25% on click-through rate.
4. **Per-cohort send-time optimization via Klaviyo's STO**: for brands with >30% international traffic, use Klaviyo's Send Time Optimization (available on Email + SMS Marketing plan or higher) to send each email at the customer's local time-of-day instead of a flat 4h post-view. Expected lift: 5–12% on open rate.
5. **Add a 7-day retarget after the 72h email**: if the customer has not purchased within 7 days of the original `Viewed Product` event, send a 4th email ("Just checking — [Product] is still here" with a per-cohort discount or social-proof block). Expected lift: 5–10% on browse-abandon revenue (per Sailor Royale 2024 + Omnisend 2024 benchmarks).
6. **Add a "viewed product 2+ times" flow**: a customer who views the same product 2+ times in 7 days without adding to cart is a strong intent signal. Create a flow that fires on the 2nd view with a 1-email "Saw you came back to [Product] — here's why others love it" + a 7-day retarget if no add-to-cart. Expected lift: 10–20% on browse-abandon CVR for the high-intent segment.
7. **BFCM browse-abandon with per-day discount ladder**: instead of a flat 10% off throughout BFCM, ramp the discount by day — Nov 25–30: 10% off, Dec 1–15: 15% off, Dec 16–24: 20% off. Verify: discount-stacking guard still holds (1 per 30d). Expected lift: 15–25% on BFCM browse-abandon revenue.
8. **AI-generated subject lines via Klaviyo AI + Attentive AI + Iterable AI**: use the AI subject-line generator on each of the 5 flows' first email. Compare against the static A/B test for 30 days, lock the winner, then move to AI body content. Expected lift: 3–8% on open rate.
