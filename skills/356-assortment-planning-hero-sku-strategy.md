## What "best in class" looks like

The 6-pillar framework is built so that each pillar's output composes into the next, and so that the operator's weekly merch-meeting + monthly catalog-tier-rebalance + quarterly assortment-rollout all run off the same 3-tier hierarchy.

### Pillar 1 — Hero-SKU selection engine

The per-SKU-scoring engine that ranks every active SKU against 5 weighted dimensions and produces the operator's "hero-roster" of top 5–20 SKUs. Without this engine, merch-mix is operator-head-only tribal knowledge and the homepage + paid-ads + email + cross-sell each surface different "best SKUs" (the canonical inconsistency anti-pattern per Triple-Whale-2026 + Nosto-2026 + Klaviyo-2026 + Algolia-2026 benchmarks).

#### Per-SKU-scoring rubric (5 dimensions × weights summing to 100)

| Dimension | Default weight | What it measures | Source | Tier-1 brand score | Tier-2 brand score | Tier-3 brand score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 — 90-day revenue** | 25% | `orders × AOV × margin` over trailing 90d | Triple Whale per-SKU report | ≥$10k = hero | $3k–$10k = halo | <$3k = filler |
| **D2 — Per-customer repeat-purchase rate** | 20% | repeat-purchase rate among customers who bought this SKU | Triple Whale cohort-LTV + Klaviyo | ≥40% = hero | 20%–40% = halo | <20% = filler |
| **D3 — Review count + rating** | 15% | `reviews_count × avg_rating` | Yotpo / Loox / Junip / Stamped | ≥50 reviews AND ≥4.5★ = hero | 10–50 reviews OR ≥4.0★ = halo | <10 reviews = filler |
| **D4 — Margin × velocity** | 25% | `(gross_margin % × orders_90d)` | Triple Whale per-SKU-margin + per-SKU-revenue | ≥70% margin AND ≥50 orders = hero | 50%–70% margin = halo | <50% margin = filler |
| **D5 — Hero-creative-fit** | 15% | number of AI-generated ad-creative variants with ROAS ≥3.0× | Moby / AdCreative.ai + Move #10 | ≥3 variants ≥3.0× ROAS = hero | 1–3 variants = halo | 0 variants = filler |

**Hero-SKU selection rubric:** top-scoring 5–20 SKUs become the **hero tier**. Score 50–80% of top becomes **halo tier**. Below becomes **filler tier**. Score thresholds calibrated per Shopify-Discovery-2026 + Nosto-2026 + Bloomreach-2026 benchmarks.

#### The 5-dimension rationale

- **D1 90-day revenue** is the **commercial signal** — a SKU that has driven $10k+ revenue in 90d is doing something right (creative + fit + price). Hero-SKUs by revenue are the "safe picks."
- **D2 repeat-purchase rate** is the **LTV signal** — a SKU with 40%+ repeat-purchase rate is the LTV anchor for the catalog. Without D2, the hero-roster over-indexes on one-time-buyer-SKUs and under-indexes on the LTV-drivers (the canonical "we-feature-our-bestseller-but-the-bestseller-has-15%-repeat-vs-our-mid-tier-product-with-45%-repeat-and-LTV-is-leaking" anti-pattern per Triple-Whale-LTV-2026 benchmarks).
- **D3 review count + rating** is the **trust signal** — a SKU with 50+ reviews AND ≥4.5★ rating converts at 25–45% higher than a no-review SKU on the same PDP (Yotpo-2026 + Loox-2026 + Junip-2026 benchmarks). Hero-SKUs by review signal are the "high-converting-picks."
- **D4 margin × velocity** is the **profit signal** — the SKU that drives $5k revenue at 30% margin makes $1.5k gross-profit; the SKU that drives $3k revenue at 70% margin makes $2.1k gross-profit. The latter is the **profit hero** even if revenue is lower. Without D4, the hero-roster over-indexes on revenue-but-not-profit and the LTV/margin-mix drifts (the canonical "our-bestseller-is-our-least-profitable-SKU-and-our-margin-erodes-by-12%-per-quarter" anti-pattern per Triple-Whale-Margin-2026 + Move #103 benchmarks).
- **D5 hero-creative-fit** is the **paid-acquisition signal** — a SKU with ≥3 ad-creative variants running at ROAS ≥3.0× is the **paid-acquisition-ready** hero; without D5, the homepage + paid-ads feature different SKUs and the creative-team-builds-creatives-for-SKUs-the-homepage-doesn't-feature (the canonical "marketing-builds-creatives-for-our-bestseller-but-the-homepage-features-our-newest-SKU-and-CAC-doubles" anti-pattern per Moby-2026 + AdCreative.ai-2026 + Move #10 + Move #7 benchmarks).

#### The canonical hero-roster composition

A healthy $3M-GMV DTC brand's hero-roster should be **8–12 SKUs** (15–25% of catalog). Composition:

| Hero-roster slot | Tier-1 (best) brand | Tier-2 (typical) brand | Tier-3 (red flag) brand |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hero-SKUs by 90-day revenue** | 4–6 | 2–4 | <2 |
| **Hero-SKUs by repeat-purchase-rate** | 2–3 | 1–2 | <1 |
| **Hero-SKUs by review signal** | 2–3 | 1–2 | 0 |
| **Hero-SKUs by margin × velocity** | 3–4 | 1–2 | <1 |
| **Hero-SKUs by creative-fit** | 2–3 | 1–2 | 0 |
| **Total hero-roster** | **8–12 SKUs** | **6–8 SKUs** | **<4 SKUs (or 1 SKU dominates 50%+ revenue)** |

**Red-flag Tier-3 brand:** 1 SKU drives 50%+ of total revenue → catalog-concentration-risk. Move #356 Pillar 5 addresses this with the **catalog-tier-rollout-cadence** + **per-SKU-cannibalization-detection**.

#### Per-SKU scoring output: the "merch-mix board"

```

SKU-002     |  85    |  90       |  85         |  78                |  80         | 84    | HERO
```

The merch-mix board is the **single source of truth** for which SKUs go to homepage / paid ads / email / cross-sell / loyalty reward / international catalog.

### Pillar 2 — 3-tier catalog hierarchy

Once the hero-roster is set, every active SKU falls into one of three tiers. The 3-tier hierarchy lets the operator budget merch-attention (collection-page real-estate, paid-creative-budget, email-feature-slot, post-purchase-cross-sell-slot, loyalty-reward-slot) across the catalog.

| Tier | Definition | # of SKUs (% of catalog) | Channel-role | Budget share | Margin profile |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HERO** | top 5–20 by Pillar 1 score | 8–15% of catalog | Featured on homepage / paid-ads / welcome-flow #3 / post-purchase-cross-sell / VIP-reward-catalog | 60–70% of merch-attention | High margin (60–80%) + high velocity |
| **HALO** | next 20–40 by Pillar 1 score | 25–35% of catalog | Featured on collection-page / category-page / search-results / browse-abandon / replenishment-flow | 25–35% of merch-attention | Mixed margin (45–65%) + medium velocity |
| **FILLER** | bottom 50% by Pillar 1 score | 50–65% of catalog | Available via search / deep-categories / sale-catalog / dead-stock-clearance | 5–10% of merch-attention | Variable margin (30–60%) + low velocity |

**Canonical 3-tier distribution for a healthy $3M-GMV DTC brand:**
- **Hero: 8–12 SKUs** driving **50–65% of revenue** (per Triple-Whale-2026 + Nosto-2026 benchmarks).
- **Halo: 25–40 SKUs** driving **25–35% of revenue**.
- **Filler: 50–100 SKUs** driving **5–15% of revenue**.

**Red-flag distribution:** 1 SKU drives 50%+ revenue (catalog-concentration-risk) OR 100+ SKUs drive <5% revenue (catalog-bloat). Move #356 Pillar 5's per-SKU-cannibalization-detection identifies these red-flags.

### Pillar 3 — Per-cohort affinity merch-engine

The same hero-roster doesn't apply to every cohort. Per-cohort affinity is the engine that decides **which SKU each cohort sees** on the homepage / in the welcome-flow / in the post-purchase-cross-sell / in the loyalty-reward-catalog.

|---|---|---|---|
| **C1 — LTV tier** | High (top 20%) / Medium / Low (bottom 20%) / New-customer / Lapsed | Per-tier purchase pattern | Triple Whale cohort-LTV + Smile tier |

### Pillar 5 — Catalog tiering + per-SKU cannibalization + dead-stock flagging + reorder-vs-discontinue

The 3-tier hierarchy + per-cohort affinity + per-channel role-mapping surface the per-SKU cannibalization + dead-stock decisions. Without Pillar 5, the catalog bloat accumulates and the operator carries 100+ filler-SKUs that consume inventory-budget, warehouse-space, and marketing-attention without driving revenue (the canonical "we-have-200-SKUs-but-15-drive-80%-of-revenue-and-the-other-185-are-warehouse-and-marketing-dead-weight" anti-pattern per Blue-Yonder-2026 + Revionics-2026 + Oracle-Retail-2026 + Move #86 dead-stock-liquidation benchmarks).

#### Per-SKU cannibalization detection

For every active SKU, compute the **cannibalization-index**:

```
cannibalization_index = (orders_SKU_X_in_period) / (orders_SKUs_in_category_in_period) × 100
```

If SKU_X cannibalization_index is **<5%** for 3+ consecutive months AND the SKU is **<hero-tier** AND has **<10 reviews**, flag for **discontinue** (or for **discontinuation-review** if it's a new SKU <6 months old).

If SKU_X cannibalization_index is **<10%** for 6+ consecutive months AND has **<30 reviews**, flag for **markdown** (per Move #13 markdown-management-on-aging-inventory).

If SKU_X is **hero-tier** AND a NEW-SKU launches in same category, monitor for **post-launch-cannibalization** — if the new-SKU cannibalizes the hero's revenue by ≥15%, flag for **merch-mix-rebalance** (hero-promotion-down OR new-SKU-deprioritization).

#### Dead-stock flagging

For every filler-tier SKU, compute **inventory-age-in-days**. If inventory-age >180 days AND no orders in trailing-90d, flag for **markdown-clearance** (per Move #86 dead-stock-liquidation benchmarks).

#### Reorder vs discontinue decision engine

For every active SKU with **<5 orders in trailing-90d**, run the **reorder-vs-discontinue-decision**:

| Question | If YES | If NO |
|---|---|---|
| Is the SKU in the hero-or-halo tier? | Consider markdown-clearance OR marketing-push | Discontinue |
| Does the SKU have ≥10 reviews OR ≥$1k LTV-per-buyer? | Consider markdown-clearance | Discontinue |
| Is the SKU a strategic halo (introduces customer to other SKUs)? | Keep + cross-merch | Discontinue |
| Does the SKU have a replenishment cadence (subscription)? | Keep + replenish | Discontinue |

The **discontinue-recommendation-output** feeds the monthly-merch-meeting + Move #86 dead-stock-liquidation workflow.

### Pillar 6 — Cadence + meeting engine + monthly board-pack + quarterly catalog-tier-rebalance

Move #356 ships as a **cadence-engine** that runs the 3-tier hierarchy + per-cohort affinity + per-channel role-mapping on a recurring cadence. Without the cadence-engine, the hero-roster goes stale in 30 days and the operator reverts to tribal knowledge.

#### Weekly merch-meeting (60 minutes)

| Time | Topic | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 min | Review last week's hero-roster performance (CVR + AOV + revenue per hero-SKU) | Hero-roster delta-list |
| 10–25 min | New-SKU review (any new-SKU launched last week? Add to halo-or-filler?) | New-SKU tier-decision |
| 25–40 min | Cohort-affinity check (any cohort-affinity drift detected?) | Cohort-affinity delta-list |
| 40–55 min | Dead-stock + cannibalization flag (any new discontinue-candidates?) | Discontinue-list |
| 55–60 min | Next-week hero-roster + per-channel role-mapping publish | Next-week merch-mix-board |

#### Monthly board-pack (1-page PDF + dashboard)

The monthly board-pack reports:
1. **Hero-roster composition** (8–12 SKUs by tier + score)
2. **Per-channel hero-performance** (revenue + CVR + AOV per channel per hero-SKU)
3. **Per-cohort-affinity-match-rate** (% of cohort-affinity-matched-merch-decisions)
4. **Cannibalization + dead-stock flag count** (current + trend)
5. **Catalog-tier-rebalance recommendations** (1 SKU move-up-to-hero, 2 SKUs move-down-from-hero, etc.)

#### Quarterly catalog-tier-rebalance (4-hour working-session)

Every quarter, the operator runs a **catalog-tier-rebalance**:
1. **Re-score every SKU** with the latest 90-day data
2. **Identify new-hero-candidates** (next 2–4 SKUs from halo)
3. **Identify hero-decline-candidates** (hero-SKUs that dropped score by ≥15%)
4. **Identify discontinue-candidates** (filler-SKUs with 3+ months of <5% cannibalization-index)
5. **Update the per-cohort affinity matrix** with last-quarter cohort-data
6. **Publish the new merch-mix-board + per-channel role-mapping**

## Common pitfalls (18 from real builds)

1. **Ship-without-Pillar-1-per-SKU-scoring** — operator picks heroes by gut, not by 5-dimension score. Hero-roster over-indexes on revenue-but-not-profit; margin erodes by 12–20% per quarter. **Fix:** ship Pillar 1 first; the per-SKU score is the single source of truth.

2. **No D2 repeat-purchase-rate dimension in the score** — operator picks heroes by 90-day revenue alone. Hero-roster over-indexes on one-time-buyer-SKUs; LTV leaks. **Fix:** D2 weight ≥20% in the rubric; LTV-by-SKU from Triple Whale.

3. **No D4 margin × velocity dimension** — operator picks heroes by revenue alone. Bestseller is least-profitable SKU; margin erodes. **Fix:** D4 weight ≥25%; cross-check against Move #103 per-SKU-profit.

4. **No D5 hero-creative-fit dimension** — homepage + paid-ads feature different SKUs. Marketing builds creatives for SKUs the homepage doesn't feature. **Fix:** D5 weight ≥15%; require ≥3 ROAS-positive creative variants per hero.

5. **Pillar 2 hero-tier-budget-share under 60%** — operator spreads merch-attention across 50 SKUs. CVR drops 15–25%. **Fix:** hero-tier budget share ≥60% (per Shopify-Discovery-2026 + Nosto-2026 benchmarks).

6. **No Pillar 3 per-cohort affinity matrix** — every cohort sees the same hero-roster. 25-cohort × 8-hero-SKU = 200-cohort-affinity-leaks. **Fix:** build the 25-cohort matrix in Phase 3; publish per-cohort hero-SKU-slot.

7. **Pillar 3 cohort-buckets too coarse** — operator uses "new-vs-returning" only, ignores LTV-tier + acquisition-source + device + region. 5-of-25 cohort-affinities addressed. **Fix:** all 5 cohort dimensions × 5 buckets = 25 cohorts.

8. **Pillar 4 homepage features the wrong 6–8 SKUs** — operator uses newest-SKUs because they're "fresh." CVR drops 8–18%. **Fix:** homepage slot count 6–8; hero-SKU-source = Pillar 1 score, not "newest."

9. **Pillar 4 email welcome-flow #3 features bestseller** — operator picks the #1 revenue SKU. New-customer cohort doesn't convert because bestseller lacks reviews + first-time-fit. CVR drops 30–45%. **Fix:** welcome-flow #3 hero-SKU = C5a first-time-buyer cohort (≥50 reviews AND ≥4.5★).

10. **Pillar 4 paid-ads features newest-SKU** — operator uses "what's hot this week." New-SKU has zero reviews, CAC doubles. **Fix:** paid-ads hero-SKU = D5 hero-creative-fit (≥3 ROAS-positive variants).

11. **Pillar 5 cannibalization detection threshold too aggressive** — operator flags SKU as "cannibalizing" with <5% cannibalization-index. Reorder-vs-discontinue runs on 50 SKUs and operator discontinues profitable ones. **Fix:** cannibalization threshold ≥5% AND <10 reviews AND 3+ consecutive months.

12. **Pillar 5 dead-stock flag too lenient** — operator ignores 200+-day inventory-age filler-SKUs. Warehouse-bloat accumulates. **Fix:** 180-day inventory-age AND <10 orders in trailing-90d = dead-stock-flag.

13. **Pillar 6 weekly-merch-meeting skipped** — operator assumes "the merch-mix-board is set." Hero-roster goes stale in 30 days. CVR drops 12–25% by month 3. **Fix:** weekly-merch-meeting 60 min, every Monday.

14. **Pillar 6 monthly-board-pack skipped** — operator doesn't review per-channel hero-performance. Cannibalization + dead-stock accumulate silently. **Fix:** monthly-board-pack + per-channel hero-performance report.

15. **Pillar 6 quarterly-rebalance skipped** — operator never re-scores. Hero-roster becomes stale-by-definition. **Fix:** quarterly-rebalance 4-hour working-session.

16. **Move #356 ships without Move #6 (attribution)** — operator can't measure per-SKU-LTV. D1, D2, D4 are blind. **Fix:** ship Move #6 first; require Triple Whale per-SKU-LTV-reporting configured.

17. **Move #356 ships without Move #9 + Move #10 (PDP + A/B)** — hero-SKU features the wrong PDP-UX. Hero-SKU CVR drops 20–35%. **Fix:** ship Move #9 + Move #10 first.

18. **Move #356 ships without Move #13 (promo-calendar)** — promotional hero-roster conflicts with merch-mix-board. Discount-fatigue erodes margin. **Fix:** ship Move #13 first; cross-reference Move #356 hero-roster in Move #13 promo-planning.
