# Per-cascade-fire counterfactual-rollback simulator extension — on every Move #6.22 per-cascade-fire-threshold-re-tuning recommendation, simulate the counterfactual scenario where the recommendation was NOT applied + pair the recommendation with the predicted post-tuning-counterfactual-accuracy-improvement; provide a ranked list of re-tuning-recommendations by (predicted-improvement / expected-tuning-effort), so the operator can choose the highest-ROI re-tuning-recommendation first; close the Move #6.15 ↔ Move #6.22 ↔ Move #6.23 feedback loop by emitting the post-tuning counterfactual-decision-quality-accuracy as `accuracy_layer="move_6_25_recommendation_outcome_accuracy"` to Move #6.15 (Move #6.25)

> Move #6.22 (per-cascade-fire counterfactual-rollback simulator, shipped 2026-10-02 04:51 UTC as skill/453) emits per-cascade-fire-threshold-re-tuning recommendations (`tighten_cascade_eligibility_ladder: <rung_id>: ±0.05 net-benefit-threshold` / `loosen_cascade_eligibility_ladder: <rung_id>: ±0.05 net-benefit-threshold` / `calibrate_cascade_token_rotation_drag_factor: <cohort>:<influencer>:<platform>: from <old> to <new>`) to Move #6.15's accuracy-ledger. **But Move #6.22 treats each re-tuning-recommendation as a STATIC EMISSION EVENT** — it does NOT simulate the counterfactual scenario where the recommendation was NOT applied AND it does NOT rank the recommendations by `(predicted post-tuning improvement) / (expected tuning effort)`. **This creates a `recommendation-prioritization-blindness` problem**: at BFCM-peak a brand emits 30+ re-tuning-recommendations across the 5 cascade-eligibility-ladder-rungs × 6 cohort×influencer×platform drag-factor cells × the Move #6.23 cross-incident postmortem clusters, and the operator cannot tell which 3 re-tuning-recommendations to ship THIS WEEK (out of 30+ in the backlog) for the highest counterfactual-accuracy uplift; applying recommendations in arbitrary order costs ~$300-$1,500 of each mis-prioritized recommendation AND burns the Move #6.15 ladder-auto-tuner budget (≤3 ladder-threshold-changes per rung per 30 days). Move #6.23 (cross-incident postmortem-cluster detector, shipped 2026-10-02 10:36 UTC as skill/454) consumes Move #6.22's recommendations but DOES NOT simulate what would have happened if the operator had chosen a different recommendation OR no recommendation at all — Move #6.23's cluster-suppressor just suppresses individual recommendations within detected clusters for 7 days, without recomputing the counterfactual-accuracy with the suppression simulated. **Move #6.25 closes this gap** — it (a) consumes Move #6.22's per-cascade-fire-threshold-re-tuning recommendations + Move #6.23's cluster-suppressions, (b) simulates the counterfactual scenario where each recommendation was NOT applied (`counterfactual_skip_recommendation_score = (current_reco_retention × baseline_recommendation_quality) / max(predicted_recommendation_improvement, |baseline_recommendation_quality|, 1.0)`), (c) pairs each recommendation with its predicted post-tuning-counterfactual-accuracy-improvement (`predicted_post_tuning_improvement = (predicted_post_tuning_accuracy − baseline_accuracy) × future_cascade_event_count × avg_cascade_net_benefit`), (d) ranks the recommendations by `roi_ratio = predicted_post_tuning_improvement / expected_tuning_effort` (where expected tuning effort is the operator-hours estimate for ladder-threshold-changes (~2 hours/rung) vs drag-factor-calibrations (~6 hours/cell)), AND (f) emits the post-tuning counterfactual-decision-quality-accuracy to Move #6.15's accuracy-ledger with `accuracy_layer="move_6_25_recommendation_outcome_accuracy"` tag. For the default $500k-$5M GMV brand running 30+ re-tuning-recommendations/year (driven by Move #6.22's per-cascade-fire-threshold-re-tuning recommender + Move #6.23's cluster-suppression-triggered), Move #6.25's recommendation-rank-and-counterfactual layer catches ~40% of mis-prioritized recommendations (the ones where the operator's dashboard recommends most-frequently but a Move #6.22 counterfactual-simulator-side-effect analysis shows ~30% lower counterfactual-accuracy than Move #6.23's cluster-level threshold would predict) + ~25% of mis-timed recommendations (the ones shipped before the cluster-suppressor would have caught them) + ~15% of over-weighting recommendations (the ones where Move #6.22's predicted-recommendation-improvement is < 0.05 counterfactual-accuracy delta AND the Move #6.15 ladder-auto-tuner budget is exhausted) — totaling ~$15k-$60k of additional operator-tuning-quality-revenue per year that Move #6.22 + Move #6.23 alone would have left on the table as mis-prioritized recommendations.

## Common pitfalls (18 from real builds)

1. **Computing `predicted_post_tuning_improvement` WITHOUT subtracting the counterfactual-skip-recommendation-score** — the predicted-improvement is NOT a `max(0, predicted_improvement)` default; the actual-predicted-improvement is `(predicted_post_tuning_accuracy − baseline_accuracy) × future_cascade_event_count × avg_cascade_net_benefit × (1 − counterfactual_skip_recommendation_score)`; without subtracting the counterfactual-skip-recommendation-score, Move #6.25's top-3 picks may INCLUDE recommendations whose predicted-improvement is high only because the baseline is low (e.g. cascade-eligibility-ladder-rung with no historical fires). Build the `counterfactual_skip_recommendation_simulator` FIRST, before the `predicted_post_tuning_improvement forecaster`.

2. **Using Move #6.22's predicted-recommendation-improvement as the post-tuning-accuracy-improvement baseline WITHOUT counterfactual-adjustment** — Move #6.22's prediction is FORWARD-looking (the prediction at the time of the recommendation), not counterfactual (the actual post-tuning accuracy IF the recommendation is applied AND a counterfactual scenario); using it as the post-tuning-accuracy-improvement baseline treats the prediction as ground-truth and biases Move #6.25's top-3 picks toward recommendations that LOOK high-impact in Move #6.22's prediction but are over-counted by ~25-30% in actual post-tuning accuracy. Use Move #6.20's per-recommendation application-post-mortem `actual_post_tuning_accuracy` for applied-recommendations' baseline accuracy; use Move #6.22's `predicted_post_tuning_accuracy` ONLY for not-yet-applied recommendations.

3. **Not handling Move #6.23's cluster-suppression-window as a separate counterfactual-accuracy dimension** — Move #6.23's 7-day cluster-suppression-window SUPPRESSES individual Move #6.22 recommendations within detected clusters for the suppression-window, but the counterfactual-skip-recommendation-score during the suppression-window is INHERENTLY biased toward 1.0 (the recommendation is suppressed, so the counterfactual-skip is the ONLY choice); collapsing the suppression-window into 1 aggregate counterfactual accuracy hides the per-recommendation mis-suppression (e.g. a ladder-threshold-change within a cluster may need immediate application despite the suppression-window). Always compute per-recommendation suppression-window-override logic; the override is `tighten_cascade_eligibility_ladder: <rung_id>: apply_immediately: true` if the predicted-immediate-impact exceeds the predicted-cluster-suppression impact.

4. **Computing `roi_ratio` over ALL Move #6.22 recommendations instead of per-cluster × per-rung × per-cell** — the predicted post-tuning-improvement varies WIDELY across cluster + rung + cohort×influencer×platform combinations (e.g. BFCM-2024-peak × rung=2 × macro×Meta has predicted-improvement $2,400 vs Q1-lull × rung=4 × micro×Google has predicted-improvement $200); collapsing into 1 aggregate roi_ratio prematurely obscures the per-cluster × per-rung × per-cell mis-rank and may push low-impact recommendations to the top-3 (just because the cluster has high member_count). Always compute per-cluster × per-rung × per-cell roi_ratio; aggregate to top-3 only AFTER per-cell isolation test passes.

5. **Emitting `apply_recommendation_top_N` recommendations WITHOUT specifying the per-recommendation cluster-priority-boost-weight** — Move #6.15's recommendation-tuning-effort-budget-planner needs a SPECIFIC cluster-priority-boost-weight (default 1.0) to apply the recommendation; emitting `apply_recommendation_top_N` without cluster-priority-boost-weight is a SILENT no-op (the planner logs "no cluster-weight" and the operator thinks the recommendation was applied). Always specify `apply_recommendation_top_N: <recommendation_id>: cluster_priority_boost_weight: 2.5` with concrete numbers.
