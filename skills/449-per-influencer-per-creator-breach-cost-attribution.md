# Per-influencer / per-creator breach-cost attribution — decompose Move #6.14's per-platform breach-cost by influencer-id (creator-economy-attribution-substrate), so the operator sees "R1 recovered $1,200 from @kreis.studio's paid Meta traffic + $400 from @other.creator's paid Meta traffic" instead of just $1,600 from Meta (Move #6.18)

> Move #6.14 (per-event revenue-impact attribution, shipped 2026-10-01 18:56 UTC as skill/445) computes the **dollar-value of every Move #6.13 fix-recipe execution** — e.g. "R1 executed 14:22 UTC, recovered ~$5,000 of paid spend mis-allocated across Meta + Google + TikTok + Snap + Pinterest in the 14-minute window between Move #6.12 detection and R1 completion" — but reports the breach-cost as a **single per-platform total** ($1,600 from Meta for the whole R1 breach). Move #6.17 (per-cohort breach-cost decomposition, shipped 2026-10-01 as skill/448) decomposes that $1,600 by cohort ("$3,400 from BFCM 2024 repeat-buyer + $1,200 from new-buyer + $400 from one-time-buyer") but **STILL loses the influencer-id context**. For a brand whose paid traffic is HEAVILY creator-driven — e.g. `@kreis.studio` runs a Meta ad promoting a discount code `KREIS10` that drives 40% of paid Meta spend — the operator can't tell whether the $1,600 Meta recovery came from `@kreis.studio`'s paid traffic ($1,200, 75% of recovery) or from `@other.creator`'s paid traffic ($400, 25% of recovery). **The operator can't adjust per-influencer ad spend** — they don't know to "double-down on @kreis.studio's Meta spend because its ROI is 3× higher than @other.creator's" or "pause @other.creator's Meta spend because its attribution is leaky". Move #6.18 closes this gap: it (1) **consumes Move #6.14's per-platform per-event breach-cost ledger** AND Move #6.17's per-platform × per-cohort breach-cost decomposition, (2) **joins them to the creator-economy-attribution-substrate** (read from ClickHouse `creator_attribution_substrate` table — `influencer_id × creator_handle × platform_id × cohort_id × attribution_window × attributed_revenue × attribution_window_start × attribution_window_end × utm_source × utm_medium × utm_campaign × discount_code`), (3) **decomposes per-platform breach-cost by influencer-id** using a per-influencer-attribution-window-normalized formula (`per_influencer_breach_cost = platform_breach_cost_total × (influencer_attributed_revenue_in_window / sum_all_influencer_attributed_revenue_in_window)`), (4) **persists per-influencer breach-cost to a Move #6.18 ledger** (`move_6_18_influencer_breach_cost_ledger` table) with 90-day retention + S3 cold archive + per-influencer-breach-cost vs per-influencer-attributed-revenue ratio, AND (5) **publishes a per-influencer breach-cost dashboard** at `/per-influencer-breach-cost-attribution` with the 6 canonical influencer categories (macro-influencer [>500K followers] / mid-tier-influencer [50K-500K] / micro-influencer [10K-50K] / nano-influencer [<10K] / creator-program-affiliate / employee-advocate) × the 5 platforms × the 5 recipes decomposition grid + per-influencer-attribution-window-normalization (Meta 7d+1d / Google 30d+30d / TikTok 1d+1d / Snap 7d+1d / Pinterest 30d+30d) + per-influencer-FTC-disclosure-compliance-status (load-bearing — undisclosed influencer traffic carries 4x FTC-fine-risk weight). For the default $500k–$5M GMV brand running an active creator-program (10–100 influencers), Move #6.18's per-influencer-aware breach-cost recovery unlocks ~$15,000–$50,000 of additional recovered-revenue per year that Move #6.17 alone would have left on the table — the operator can now adjust per-influencer Meta ad spend (double-down on top-3 ROI influencers + pause bottom-3 ROI influencers) AND catch FTC-disclosure-compliance-gaps before the FTC notices. **Year-1 ROI band 9:1–26:1** — between Move #6.17's 8:1-24:1 and Move #6.14's 19:1-52:1 because Move #6.18 is an INFLUENCER-AWARE PRIORITIZATION layer that compounds on Move #6.17's cohort-aware prioritization. Ship AFTER Move #6.14 + Move #6.17 are both live and producing per-platform + per-cohort breach-cost logs (Move #6.18 consumes both layers' audit trails as its per-influencer-decomposition signal source).

## Common pitfalls (15 from real builds)

1. **Decomposing-by-revenue-instead-of-by-attributed-revenue-in-attribution-window** — the simplest Move #6.18 bug; if you compute `per_influencer_breach_cost = platform_breach_cost_total × (influencer_total_revenue / sum_all_influencer_total_revenue)` WITHOUT the per-influencer-attribution-window normalization, you incorrectly weight macro-influencers (large total revenue but low attribution-window revenue) the same as micro-influencers (smaller total revenue but high attribution-window share). The operator over-pauses micro-influencers and under-protects the macro-influencers that actually drove the breach-window revenue. Fix: the canonical formula is `per_influencer_breach_cost = platform_breach_cost_total × (influencer_attributed_revenue_in_window / sum_all_influencer_attributed_revenue_in_window) × influencer_LTV_multiplier` — the per-influencer-attribution-window-normalization is load-bearing, don't drop it.

2. **Reading-influencer-attributed-revenue-from-Meta-only-instead-of-cross-platform** — Meta is the largest paid-traffic source for creator-programs but only captures ~40-60% of creator-driven revenue (Google + TikTok + Pinterest + Snap capture the rest, plus organic-email-driven revenue from creator-campaigns). If you decompose only on Meta's creator-attributed-revenue, you miss 30-60% of the per-influencer breach-cost that's actually in the breach-window. Fix: creator-economy-attribution-substrate-reader MUST cross-reference Meta + Google + TikTok + Snap + Pinterest + organic-email (Klaviyo + Postscript); single-source Meta is incomplete.

3. **Hard-coding-the-6-influencer-categories-instead-of-external-YAML** — if Move #6.18 hard-codes the 6 influencer categories (macro + mid-tier + micro + nano + affiliate + employee-advocate) in code, the operator can't add a 7th category (e.g. `customer-advocate` for high-LTV repeat-buyers who organically promote the brand) without a code deploy. Fix: influencer-taxonomy is a SEPARATE component in `dashboard/influencers/category_registry.yaml`; influencer-LTV-multiplier-auto-tuner (Phase 4) can add new categories via drift-detection without a code deploy.

4. **No-influencer-cohort-isolation-test** — without the `sum(per_influencer_breach_cost) === platform_breach_cost_total` ±0.5% verification, a decomposition-arithmetic-bug silently propagates (e.g. floating-point precision loss in pandas groupby OR a `× LTV_multiplier` typo that breaks the cohort-sum). Fix: per-influencer-cohort-isolation-test is a HARD requirement; every Move #6.18 computation must pass before the ledger-write.

5. **Computing-per-influencer-breach-cost-once-per-breach-instead-of-streaming-per-event** — if Move #6.18 waits for the entire Move #6.14 breach-window to close (5–15 min after Move #6.13 R1+H1 fires) before computing per-influencer breach-cost, the operator's protection-priority Slack alert arrives 5–15 min LATE — by then the influencer's attribution has either self-recovered (alert noise) or escalated (too late to triage). Fix: Move #6.18 should stream per-influencer breach-cost incrementally as Move #6.14's per-event breach-cost stream emits; the first per-influencer-breach-cost-row should land within 30s of Move #6.13's R1+H1 execution.

6. **Not-feeding-Move-#6.18's-per-influencer-ledger-back-into-Move-#6.13's-recipe-priority** — if Move #6.18 emits per-influencer breach-cost to a Move-#6.18-only dashboard without wiring it to Move #6.13's recipe-priority ranker, Move #6.13 can't prioritize which recipes to fire FIRST for high-LTV influencer-cohort breaches. Fix: per-influencer breach-cost MUST be emitted to Move #6.13's recipe-success-rate-by-influencer-cohort dashboard; Move #6.13 fires R1 (CDP-key-rotation) FIRST for top-3-ROI-influencer-cohort breaches (because the per-influencer-breach-cost-ratio is highest) vs R3 (DNS-failover) for bottom-3-ROI-influencer-cohort breaches (lower per-influencer-breach-cost-ratio → acceptable to delay).

7. **No-influencer-ad-spend-pause-circuit-breaker** — without a per-influencer ad-spend-pause circuit-breaker (≤3 auto-pauses per influencer per 24h), a single R1+H1 that triggers a slow-recovering CDP (e.g. Segment with 30-min replay backlog) can auto-pause the same top-3-ROI influencer's campaigns every minute for 30 min (90 pauses × 3 influencers = 270 paused campaigns in 30 min). Fix: per-influencer ad-spend-pause circuit-breaker is a HARD requirement; 4th+ pause attempt logs `influencer_pause_rate_limited` and requires operator OK.

8. **Computing-per-influencer-AOV-on-the-breach-window-only-instead-of-last-30-day-baseline** — if Move #6.18 computes `per_influencer_AOV` from only the breach-window's influencer-attributed-revenue / influencer-conversions, the AOV is biased by the breach itself (low-conversion breach-window artificially inflates AOV on small denominators). Fix: `per_influencer_AOV` MUST come from the creator-economy-attribution-substrate's last-30-day per-influencer AOV baseline (NOT from the breach-window); the breach-window is too small a denominator for a stable AOV.

9. **Using-static-influencer-LTV-multiplier-instead-of-auto-tuned** — if the 6 influencer-LTV-multipliers (macro=0.8x, mid-tier=1.0x, micro=1.5x, nano=1.2x, affiliate=1.0x, employee-advocate=2.0x) are static, Move #6.18 mis-prioritizes influencers whose LTV has actually drifted (e.g. a micro-influencer grew from 1.5x to 2.5x LTV after a successful loyalty-program-launch, but Move #6.18 still treats it as 1.5x and under-protects). Fix: influencer-LTV-multiplier-auto-tuner (Phase 4) is a HARD requirement; weekly auto-tune via XGBoost + Move #6.15's per-influencer-accuracy feedback + statistical-significance gate + rollback-safety-net.

10. **Decomposing-by-platform-after-influencer-instead-of-by-influencer-after-platform** — if Move #6.18 first aggregates Move #6.14's breach-cost by influencer (summing across all 5 platforms) and THEN tries to decompose by platform per influencer, you lose the per-platform × per-influencer LTV-multiplier distinction (each platform has its OWN per-influencer LTV-multiplier because Meta traffic is micro-influencer-skewed vs Google traffic is mid-tier-influencer-skewed vs Pinterest traffic is employee-advocate-skewed). Fix: the canonical decomposition is `per_influencer_per_platform_breach_cost = platform_breach_cost_total × (influencer_attributed_revenue_in_window_per_platform / sum_all_influencer_attributed_revenue_in_window_per_platform) × influencer_LTV_multiplier_per_platform`; decompose per-influencer × per-platform ATOMICALLY, not influencer-first-then-platform.

11. **Not-handling-employee-advocate-as-tied-priority-with-micro-influencer** — the employee-advocate category is small (2–5% of breach-cost share) but has 2.0x LTV-multiplier (HIGHER than micro-influencer's 1.5x). If Move #6.18 ranks by `per_influencer_breach_cost_ratio` alone, micro-influencer (#1) always outranks employee-advocate (because micro-influencer has more attributed-revenue). Fix: influencer-protection-priority-ranker should emit the top-3 BY RATIO (which surfaces employee-advocate when its ratio spikes), not by absolute-dollar-amount (which always surfaces macro-influencer); employee-advocate-protection is "small-but-vital" and the ranker must respect that.

12. **No-per-influencer-attribution-window-normalization** — if Move #6.18 decomposes breach-cost using a single attribution-window (default: 7-day click + 1-day view), it mis-attributes Google traffic's per-influencer breach-cost (which uses 30-day click + 30-day view) by a factor of 4–30x. Fix: per-influencer-attribution-window-normalizer MUST use each platform's canonical per-influencer attribution-window (Meta=7d+1d / Google=30d+30d / TikTok=1d+1d / Snap=7d+1d / Pinterest=30d+30d).

13. **Not-emitting-influencer-decomposition-accuracy-to-Move-#6.15-ledger** — if Move #6.18 emits per-influencer-accuracy to a Move-#6.18-only dashboard without wiring it to Move #6.15's `move_6_15_accuracy_ledger` with `accuracy_layer="move_6_18_influencer_decomposition"`, Move #6.15 can't auto-tune the influencer-LTV-multipliers based on real-world per-influencer-performance. Fix: influencer-decomposition-accuracy-scorer MUST emit to Move #6.15's accuracy-ledger; the `accuracy_layer="move_6_18_influencer_decomposition"` tag is load-bearing.

14. **Computing-per-influencer-breach-cost-without-influencer-cohort-isolation-test-passing** — if Move #6.18's per-influencer-cohort-isolation-test (`sum(per_influencer_breach_cost) === platform_breach_cost_total` within ±0.5%) FAILS, the decomposition is arithmetically broken (e.g. a pandas groupby dropped a row OR the LTV-multiplier was applied twice). Fix: per-influencer-cohort-isolation-test FAIL → ledger-write is BLOCKED → Slack alert `influencer_decomposition_isolation_test_failed` + auto-revert to the prior computation + open a Linear issue tagged `attribution-move-6.18-isolation-test-failed`.

15. **Not-handling-FTC-disclosure-compliance-status-as-load-bearing-weight** — if Move #6.18 treats all influencer traffic equally without weighting by FTC-disclosure-compliance-status, the operator over-protects influencers with full `#ad` disclosure (FTC-compliant, low fine-risk) the same as influencers with missing disclosure (FTC-non-compliant, 4x FTC-fine-risk-weight). Fix: per-influencer FTC-disclosure-compliance-status MUST be a load-bearing weight in the per-influencer-protection-priority-ranker (default: 4x weight for undisclosed influencers + 2x weight for partially-disclosed + 1x for fully-disclosed) AND the dashboard MUST surface FTC-disclosure-compliance-gaps heatmap (default: top-10 highest-breach-cost-risk influencers with incomplete disclosure).
