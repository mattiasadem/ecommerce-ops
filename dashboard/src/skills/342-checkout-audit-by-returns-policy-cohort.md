## Common pitfalls (18 from real builds)

1. **Lumping all free-returns SKUs into a single "free-returns-positive" bucket.** The canonical free-returns cohort carries materially different completion-rate-leak-rate profiles across free-returns-30-day / free-returns-60-day / free-returns-90-day (free-returns-30-day 78-90%, free-returns-60-day 75-88%, free-returns-90-day 70-85%) and requires separate fix-recommendations. Free-returns-90-day specifically carries the highest leak-rate (15-30%) among the free-returns cohorts because customers expect 30-day returns and may abandon cart when seeing 90-day-only-returns-display — a counter-fix (e.g. enable-return-window-display-engine or enable-final-sale-discount-engine). **Fix:** keep the canonical 3 free-returns-window cohorts as separate cohorts; do not collapse into "free-returns-positive" or "no-free-returns" buckets.

2. **Confusing returns-policy-cohort with review-aggregation-cohort or trust-signal-cohort.** Move #3.14 is returns-policy-cohort = which-returns-policy-is-rendered-on-the-checkout (8-cohort taxonomy); review-aggregation-cohort = which-product-reviews-aggregator-is-the-source (Trustpilot vs Yotpo vs Stamped vs Loox vs Junip vs Bazaarvoice — typically 6-12 cohorts, covered by Move #43); trust-signal-cohort = which-trust-badge-is-rendered (the canonical 12-cohort taxonomy, covered by Move #3.12). Move #3.14 ≠ Move #43 ≠ Move #3.12. **Fix:** keep the canonical 8 returns-policy-cohorts as a per-rendered-returns-policy taxonomy, NOT per-source or per-platform.

3. **Treating paid-returns-flat-fee as the same as paid-returns-percent-of-order.** Paid-returns-flat-fee = customer pays a flat fee (e.g. $7.95 per return) regardless of order value. Paid-returns-percent-of-order = customer pays a percentage of order (e.g. 10% restocking fee). Completion-rate differs (paid-returns-flat-fee 58-72% vs paid-returns-percent-of-order 42-58%) because high-AOV customers are deterred by percent-of-order fees (e.g. $50 fee on $500 order = 10% feels manageable; $50 fee on $5000 order = 1% feels manageable; but 10% on $5000 = $500 fee is a strong deterrent). **Fix:** keep paid-returns-flat-fee-cohort and paid-returns-percent-of-order-cohort as separate cohorts; do not collapse.

4. **Treating store-credit-only-returns as the same as cash-refund-returns.** Store-credit-only-returns = customer receives store-credit only (no cash refund, often with bonus-credit-engine +10-25%). Cash-refund-returns = customer receives cash refund to original payment method. Each carries different completion-rate (store-credit-only-returns 50-65% vs cash-refund-returns 78-90% on the free-returns cohorts). **Fix:** keep the canonical store-credit-only-returns cohort distinct; the canonical 8-cohort taxonomy uses store-credit-only-returns as the cohort that benefits most from enable-store-credit-bonus-engine.

5. **Treating exchange-only-returns as the same as cash-refund-returns.** Exchange-only-returns = customer can only exchange for different size/color, no refund. Cash-refund-returns = customer receives cash refund. Each carries different completion-rate-leak-rate profiles (exchange-only-returns 55-70% vs cash-refund-returns 78-90%) because exchange-process-clarity and exchange-bonus-product-engine availability affect customer-confidence. **Fix:** keep the canonical exchange-only-returns cohort distinct from cash-refund-returns; the canonical 8-cohort taxonomy uses exchange-only-returns as the cohort that benefits most from enable-exchange-process-clarity-engine.

6. **Treating final-sale-no-returns as the same as paid-returns-percent-of-order.** Final-sale-no-returns = no returns accepted (final sale, made-to-order, clearance). Paid-returns-percent-of-order = customer pays a percentage of order (typically 5-15%). Each carries different completion-rate-leak-rate profiles (final-sale-no-returns 20-38% vs paid-returns-percent-of-order 42-58%) and different LTV impact (final-sale-no-returns drives +5-15% LTV due to clearance-discount-perceived-value but loses 30-50% of cart-abandon-on-display, paid-returns-percent-of-order drives +5-12% LTV). **Fix:** keep the canonical final-sale-no-returns cohort distinct from paid-returns-percent-of-order; the canonical 8-cohort taxonomy uses final-sale-no-returns as the cohort that benefits most from enable-final-sale-discount-engine + enable-final-sale-clarity-engine.

7. **Treating returns-policy-cohort as a top-level checkout-element rather than a per-rendered-returns-policy taxonomy.** Move #3.14's returns-policy-cohort taxonomy is per-rendered-returns-policy-on-the-checkout, NOT per-checkout-element. The canonical 8 returns-policy-cohorts are: free-returns-30-day + free-returns-60-day + free-returns-90-day + paid-returns-flat-fee + paid-returns-percent-of-order + store-credit-only-returns + exchange-only-returns + final-sale-no-returns. Each cohort is rendered at a specific checkout-step (e.g. returns-policy-summary at cart-step, returns-policy-fee at shipping-step, returns-policy-process at confirmation-step). **Fix:** tag each per-returns-policy-cohort-encounter with the rendered-checkout-step, not just "checkout-level".

8. **Single-A/B-test against an aggregate returns-policy-cohort-prominence-engine rather than per-returns-policy-cohort A/B-test.** The canonical 42 per-returns-policy-cohort-fix-recommendations are per-cohort-specific (F1 enable-returns-policy-cohort-prominence-engine targets final-sale-no-returns specifically; F2 enable-returns-policy-cohort-free-returns-fee-disclosure-engine targets paid-returns-flat-fee specifically). A single A/B-test against an aggregate returns-policy-cohort-prominence-engine would dilute the per-cohort effect and miss the +15-30% completion-rate-uplift on the targeted cohort. **Fix:** run per-returns-policy-cohort A/B-tests, NOT an aggregate A/B-test.

9. **Treating returns-policy-cohort A/B-test as a low-power test.** Per-returns-policy-cohort A/B-tests require minimum-detectable-effect of 3-5% (per Move #47) and runtime of 7-21 days. Low-power A/B-tests (e.g. 100 events per cohort) produce false-positive completions. **Fix:** set per-returns-policy-cohort A/B-test minimum sample size to ≥1,000 events per cohort; runtime 7-21 days; statistical-significance threshold 95%.

10. **Ignoring returns-policy-cohort-mobile-cohort-confusion.** Mobile-cohort customers see returns-policy at 50-75% smaller render area than desktop-cohort customers (per Move #3.4 device-cohort + Triple-Whale-Returns-Cohort-2024). The returns-policy-cohort-render-event on mobile-cohort must be tagged with mobile-render-area; if not, the returns-policy-cohort-mobile-experience-gap-counter fires incorrectly. **Fix:** tag every returns-policy-cohort-render-event with mobile-render-area + mobile-touch-target-44px-engine-status.

11. **Treating returns-policy-cohort attribution-double-counting as a global issue.** Returns-policy-cohort attribution-double-counting happens when a single checkout-step-attempt is tagged with multiple returns-policy-cohorts (e.g. paid-returns-flat-fee + paid-returns-percent-of-order + final-sale-no-returns all rendering simultaneously). Triple-Whale per-returns-policy-cohort-attribution must apply canonical dedup-rules (last-touch-wins + returns-policy-cohort-priority-rank). **Fix:** apply canonical returns-policy-cohort-priority-rank (free-returns-30-day > free-returns-60-day > free-returns-90-day > paid-returns-flat-fee > store-credit-only-returns > exchange-only-returns > paid-returns-percent-of-order > final-sale-no-returns).

12. **Ignoring returns-policy-cohort-cohort-LTV-decision.** Per-returns-policy-cohort-cohort-LTV-decision differs from per-returns-policy-cohort-LTV. Returns-policy-cohort-cohort-LTV = sum(customer-LTV × cohort-weight) per-returns-policy-cohort — the canonical 50-cell matrix (per-customer-LTV-tier × per-returns-policy-cohort). Operators who only track per-returns-policy-cohort-LTV miss the cohort-mix-shift that drives +10-28% LTV-uplift. **Fix:** track per-returns-policy-cohort-cohort-LTV explicitly; route to per-cohort-audience-engine via Move #115.

13. **Returns-policy-cohort-discount-uplift-ceiling-failure.** Discount-uplift-ceiling on returns-policy-cohort = +5-15% (vs +15-30% on creator-economy-cohort per Move #3.11). Operators who apply creator-economy-cohort-discount-uplift-ceiling-engine to returns-policy-cohort over-discount and erode margin. **Fix:** apply canonical per-returns-policy-cohort-discount-uplift-ceiling (5-15% on free-returns-30-day, 8-15% on store-credit-only-returns, etc.).

14. **Returns-policy-cohort-mobile-cohort-confusion with creator-economy-cohort-mobile-cohort-confusion.** Move #3.14 mobile-cohort-confusion is render-area-driven (returns-policy-cohort-mobile-render-area); Move #3.11 mobile-cohort-confusion is creator-bio-link-driven (creator-mobile-bio-link-friction). Each requires a separate counter. **Fix:** keep the canonical 2 mobile-cohort-confusion counters separate; Move #3.14 = returns-policy-cohort-mobile-render-area-confusion-counter; Move #3.11 = creator-bio-link-mobile-confusion-counter.

15. **Ignoring returns-policy-cohort-cross-border-returns-display.** Cross-border customers see returns-policy at different render-priority than domestic customers (per Move #25 international-expansion + Move #79 cross-border-returns-operations). If not handled, the returns-policy-cohort-cross-border-returns-display-counter fires and the final-sale-no-returns cohort completion-rate drops 8-15% on cross-border (because customers don't realize cross-border returns are even harder). **Fix:** tag every returns-policy-cohort-render-event with cross-border-vs-domestic; apply canonical cross-border-returns-policy-cohort-render-priority (free-returns-30-day first, then paid-returns-flat-fee with cross-border-fee-disclosure).

16. **Returns-policy-cohort-payment-fallback-failure.** When a primary payment-method fails, the returns-policy-cohort (especially final-sale-no-returns + paid-returns-percent-of-order) may be de-prioritized in the fallback-render, leading to a payment-step-leak. **Fix:** apply canonical payment-fallback-returns-policy-cohort-priority (returns-policy is ALWAYS rendered, even in payment-fallback path).

17. **Returns-policy-cohort-return-window-display-staleness.** Returns-policy-cohort-window from Shopify-Return-Policy-API may be stale by 30-300 seconds due to webhook-delivery-lag. A customer who adds a 30-day-return-policy SKU to cart may complete checkout only to receive an email 60 seconds later showing the policy changed to final-sale — a per-checkout-step-leak. **Fix:** wire real-time returns-policy-cohort-window validation at checkout-step-entry via Shopify-Return-Policy-API + webhook-delivery-monitoring + 30-second-staleness-threshold.

18. **Global-rollback-engine-kills-high-performing-returns-policy-cohorts.** A global rollback that triggers when ANY returns-policy-cohort completion-rate drops below benchmark-floor will kill high-performing returns-policy-cohorts (e.g. free-returns-30-day + free-returns-60-day + exchange-only-returns). Per pitfall #18 the rollback-engine must be per-returns-policy-cohort-specific (per-returns-policy-cohort-rollback-decision-engine), not global. **Fix:** scope the rollback-trigger to the specific failing-returns-policy-cohort + apply canonical Move #3 + Move #3.1-Move #3.13 + Move #28 + Move #79 + Move #88 fallback only to that cohort.
