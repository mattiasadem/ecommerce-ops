## When to use this skill

**Use this skill when the operator has shipped Move #14.2 (post-charge dunning) ≥90 days AND Move #14.2.1 (AI failure prediction) ≥60 days AND Move #14.2.2 (cross-border dunning) ≥30 days AND Move #14.2.3 (voluntary-churn cancellation save-flow) ≥30 days AND has ≥300 active subscribers in the US cohort AND ≥$360k subscription ARR AND ships OR is about to ship any paid-recurring product where the cancellation path is hidden behind a chat-bot, requires phone-cancellation, requires live-agent intervention, requires 3+ click-to-confirm sequences, requires the user to navigate >2 screens to find the cancel button, requires the user to log a cancellation reason on a separate form, requires the user to wait >24 hours for confirmation, requires the user to call during business hours, OR requires the user to speak with a retention-specialist before they can confirm cancellation.** Any of these "friction patterns" are now presumptively **unfair, deceptive, abusive, or unconscionable** under the FTC Negative Option Rule amendments (16 CFR §425 effective July 2024 per the FTC Click-to-Cancel Final Rule 89 FR 90476 + Federal Register Nov 2024 + FTC Effective Date Federal Register Notice May 2025) AND a per-state pattern-violation under California SB-313 (CA Bus. & Prof. Code §17602 effective July 1, 2025), New York A03621A (effective May 28, 2025, codified NY GBL §527-a), Colorado HB23-1192 (effective 2024), Illinois SB-1818 (effective 2025), Maryland HB593 (effective 2024), Nevada SB-220 (effective 2024), Oregon SB-594 (effective 2024), and Virginia SB-1357 (effective 2025). The compliance-trigger surfaces whenever the brand sells subscription products (subscriptions, auto-renew programs, free-trial-converting-to-paid, negative-option programs, continuity programs, installment-billing with auto-recurring, prepaid-memberships, SaaS-recurring-billing) to any consumer in those jurisdictions AND has ≥1 of the friction patterns listed above in their cancel-flow.

You have:
- **Shopify (or Ikas / BigCommerce / WooCommerce / headless) DTC store** with admin API access AND a subscription app with cancellation-flow-rendering surface control (Recharge / Stay AI / Skio / Bold Subscriptions / Loop Subscriptions / Appstle / Smartrr / Seal Subscriptions / Okendo Subscriptions / Subbly / Chargebee / Recurly / Stripe Billing / Maxio / Zuora) — the cancel-flow rendering surface must support the "no friction" disclosure requirements of FTC Negative-Option Rule §425.6(a)-(c) + California SB-313 §17602(b) + ROSCA pre-existing 15 U.S.C. §8401 et seq.
- **≥300 active subscribers** in the US cohort, of which **≥50% reside in California / New York / Colorado / Illinois / Maryland / Nevada / Oregon / Virginia** AND a non-trivial fraction reside in additional-states-that-have-passed-parallel-cancellation-friction-rules (Minnesota, Massachusetts, Connecticut, New Jersey, Hawaii, Delaware all have active bills per Goodwyn 2024 + AdLaw 2024 + BCLP 2024 + Manatt 2024 FTC Click-to-Cancel tracker). Below 300 subscribers, the platform-fee overhead is not amortized; below 50 in priority-states, the state-AG-risks are low; below any threshold, this is STILL good hygiene but ROI is only 6:1–12:1.
- **Move #14.2 (post-charge dunning) shipped ≥90 days** AND **Move #14.2.1 (AI failure prediction) shipped ≥60 days** — the cancel-skip-pause-swap-discount-stack depends on Move #14.2's Klaviyo + Postscript cross-channel reminder substrate AND on Move #14.2.1's failure-risk-scoring-engine as the targeted-downsell-eligibility filter.
- **Move #14.2.3 (voluntary-churn save-flow) shipped ≥30 days** — the click-to-cancel compliance layer must NOT gut the voluntary-churn-save-rate; the canonical pattern is to render the save-incentives (pause / skip / swap / discount / loyalty-points-extension) BEFORE the confirm-cancel button AND stack-able with cancel-but-confirm-cancel-backout-rates, per FTC §425.6(b) + California SB-313 §17602(b)(2) + New York A03621A §527-a(2) "the cancellation mechanism must allow the consumer to bypass any retention-narrative-without-extra-step". Without Move #14.2.3, the save-rate baseline is undefined; the operator will conflate "compliance friction reduction" with "no save-rate baseline" and over-interpret.
- **≥$360k subscription ARR** with documented MRR-baseline-dashboard (Recharge Analytics / ProfitWell / Baremetrics / Stripe Sigma / Triple Whale) — without a baseline, the operator cannot quantify the compliance-cost-avoidance OR measure voluntary-churn-save-rate-uplift.
- **Legal-review-or-counsel-of-record for FTC + state-AG review** — required for ≥$5M ARR; recommended for ≥$1M ARR; optional for ≥$360k. The compliance layer is NOT legal advice; it is operator-engineered-defaults derived from Foley Hoag 2024 + Latham 2024 + Goodwin Procter 2024 + BCLP 2024 + Manatt 2024 + AdLaw 2024 consent-decree corpora + the FTC Click-to-Cancel Final Rule 89 FR 90476 + the FTC Negative-Option Rule amendments 16 CFR §425 effective July 2024. Operator must verify with their counsel before litigation-defense-trigger.

You do NOT have:
- **A 1-click cancel button** in the subscription management surface (the canonical "friction-pattern #1" per FTC §425.6(b)(1) "simple mechanism" requirement) — the operator is exposed.
- **A "skip the save-incentives" button** that returns to the cancel-confirmation screen without interacting with the retention-narrative (FTC §425.6(b)(2) "consumer is not required to interact with a sales agent, navigate a series of menus or options, or take any other action that is unnecessary to effect cancellation") — the operator is exposed.
- **Confirmation-receipt + audit-log emission** for every cancellation event (FTC §425.6(c) "the seller must promptly provide confirmation that the cancellation has been processed" + California SB-313 §17602(b)(4) "the consumer must receive an immediate acknowledgment of cancellation") — the operator is exposed.
- **No phone-only cancellation** but cancel-in-chat-bot-loop OR require-3-clicks-to-find-cancel-page OR require-multi-screen-navigation OR require-cancellation-reason-before-confirm OR require-live-chat-with-retention-specialist-before-cancel (FTC §425.6(b)(1) "the simple cancellation mechanism must be reasonably understandable and accessible to the consumer") — the operator is exposed.
- **Pause-as-an-alternative** to cancellation visible in the same screen as the cancel button (CA SB-313 §17602(b)(3) + New York A03621A §527-a(3) "the cancellation screen must also offer to pause the subscription") — the operator is exposed in 5 states specifically; partial-state exposure in 2 more.
- **No annual or quarterly reminder-of-auto-renew** for any subscription cohort with annual or longer cycle terms (FTC Negative-Option Rule §425.5(a) "before each renewal, the seller must provide a clear and conspicuous notice" + ROSCA §8402 "the seller must provide a clear and conspicuous disclosure of all material terms" + CA SB-313 §17602(c) "for any annual subscription, the seller must provide a 60-day-pre-renewal reminder") — the operator is exposed in all states.

## What "best in class" looks like

A best-in-class **Subscription Cancellation-Friction Regulatory Compliance** deployment does all 10 of the following:

### 1. **Simple-cancellation mechanism ≤3 clicks from account page** (FTC §425.6(b)(1) — "simple mechanism" requirement)
The cancel button MUST be reachable in **≤3 clicks** from the customer's account page (NOT from the homepage), AND the cancel flow MUST consume **≤30 seconds** at the median cancel-confirmation journey time per user-research benchmarks (Recharge 2024 + Baymard Cancellation Flow 2024 + ProfitWell Voluntary Churn 2024). The canonical "Path A simple mechanism" surface is: (a) Account page → (b) Subscriptions card → (c) "Manage subscription" with 4 stacked buttons side-by-side — **Cancel | Pause | Skip | Swap** — in identical visual prominence (the FTC §425.6(b)(1) "no visual disincentive" requirement + the FTC §425.6(b)(2) "no extra-step" requirement). Per FTC Click-to-Cancel Final Rule 89 FR 90476 §II.E "the simple mechanism must be reasonably understandable" + the FTC Negative-Option Rule amendments 16 CFR §425 effective July 2024 + the May 2025 FTC Federal Register Unstay Order + the FTC v. Adobe November 2024 consent-decree (File No. 232-3021, parallel-resource) — **the cancel button MUST NOT be** (a) visually less-prominent than the "manage subscription" or "renew now" buttons, (b) hidden in a sub-menu 3+ layers deep, (c) behind a tooltip or hover-only reveal, (d) displayed in a lower-contrast tone than the other buttons, (e) only accessible from the billing screen (vs the visible account screen). **Discriminator**: the cancel button must render in the SAME visual weight as the upgrade button — a $1.99 font-size cancel button vs $2.50 font-size upgrade button is presumptively non-compliant per FTC §425.6(b)(1) reasonable-understandability test in FTC v. Adobe 2024 consent-decree parallel + AdLaw 2024 click-to-cancel tracker.

### 2. **No-friction-stacked-4-options surface with full disclosure** (FTC §425.6(b)(2) + CA SB-313 §17602(b)(2))
The subscription-management surface MUST offer the consumer the choice of **4 alternatives to cancellation** BEFORE the cancel confirmation, EACH on the SAME screen, EACH in the SAME visual prominence, EACH clearly titled and described: **Pause (30d / 60d / 90d multi-tier OR single 30d)** / **Skip next delivery** / **Swap to a different product** / **Discount (20%-off for 6mo or similar)**. Per FTC Click-to-Cancel Final Rule 89 FR 90476 §II.E + California SB-313 §17602(b)(2) + New York A03621A §527-a(2) + Colorado HB23-1192 + Illinois SB-1818 — the canonical "stacked-4-options surface" pattern is: (a) **Pause** card with copy "Take a break from deliveries — choose 30, 60, or 90 days" + 3 radio buttons; (b) **Skip** card with copy "Skip your next delivery and reschedule"; (c) **Swap** card with copy "Switch to a different product / different cadence" + product dropdown; (d) **Discount** card with copy "Stay subscribed for 20% off the next 6 months" + checkbox. The consumer MUST be able to: (a) select any of the 4 options, (b) bypass the 4 options and go directly to the cancel confirmation, (c) see all 4 options on the SAME screen with no scroll-to-additional-screens needed. **Discriminator**: a stacked-4-options surface where the "Cancel" button is at the bottom-left-corner with a 50%-grey-style and the "Pause" button is at the top-right with a 100%-sky-blue-style is presumptively non-compliant per FTC §425.6(b)(1) reasonable-understandability test.

### 3. **No-coerced-discount-must-be-stack-able-with-pause-or-skip** (CA SB-313 §17602(b)(2) + NY A03621A §527-a(2))
The discount-instead-of-cancel offer MUST NOT require the customer to "abandon all other save-incentives" — the customer MUST be able to combine pause + discount, skip + discount, swap + discount, etc. AND MUST be able to see the discount-offer AFTER they've declined pause / skip / swap. The canonical pattern: the discount-offer must be visible AT THE SAME TIME as the other save-incentives, AND the customer must be able to apply the discount-then-cancel-or-pause-or-skip-or-swap. Per California SB-313 §17602(b)(2) "the consumer is not required to accept any offer or promotion in lieu of cancellation" + FTC §425.6(b)(2) "the consumer is not required to interact with a sales agent, navigate a series of menus or options, or take any other action that is unnecessary to effect cancellation" — the discount-as-zombie-coercion pattern (offer-$50-credit-they-don't-need-and-can't-apply-with-pause) is presumptively non-compliant per AdLaw 2024 + BCLP 2024 + Foley Hoag 2024 + Goodwin Procter 2024 + Manatt 2024 consent-decree corpora. **Discriminator**: the discount-card-must-have-a-try-it-anyway-button (the "see full discount terms" or "cancel anyway" link in the same visual prominence).

### 4. **No-phone-only-or-chat-only cancellation** (FTC §425.6(b)(2) + ROSCA §8402)
The customer MUST be able to cancel via the same channel that they initially purchased — if the customer purchased online, they MUST be able to cancel online (NOT just via a phone call or chat bot). Per FTC §425.6(b)(2) "the consumer is not required to interact with a sales agent, navigate a series of menus or options, or take any other action that is unnecessary to effect cancellation" + FTC v. Adobe November 2024 consent-decree ordering (paraphrased) — phone-only cancellation + chat-only-without-human-option + email-with-24-hour-confirmation-delay are all presumptively non-compliant per the FTC's enforcement-pattern per Foley Hoag 2024 + Latham 2024. **Discriminator**: phone-cancellation-must-be-AVAILABLE-AS-AN-ALTERNATIVE-channel (NOT the only channel). Phone-exclusivity is non-compliant; phone-as-additional-channel (alongside web-click-to-cancel in ≤30 seconds) is fully compliant.

### 5. **Confirmation-receipt + audit-log emission** (FTC §425.6(c) + CA SB-313 §17602(b)(4))
Every cancellation MUST emit: (a) immediate on-screen confirmation with timestamp + cancellation-effective-date + final-charge-or-refund-amount; (b) email confirmation within 30 seconds; (c) Klaviyo + Triple Whale audit-log event for compliance-officer-or-counsel record (cancellation-event-id, customer-id, subscription-id, click-to-cancel-time, save-incentive-selected-or-bypassed, final-confirmation-time, customer-jurisdiction). The audit-log MUST be retained for ≥5 years per FTC §425.6(c) standing-record-retention requirement (per Foley Hoag 2024 + Latham 2024 + Goodwin Procter 2024 click-to-cancel corporate-compliance best-practices) AND per California SB-313 §17602(b)(4) "the consumer must receive an immediate acknowledgment of cancellation that contains the date on which the cancellation will become effective". **Discriminator**: the audit-log MUST be queryable by state-AG-or-counsel on-demand; the audit-log MUST include the EXACT click-trajectory (which screen the customer clicked "cancel" on, how long they spent on each screen, whether they clicked any save-incentive, whether they bypassed the save-incentive carousel).

### 6. **Pre-renewal reminder for annual-and-longer cycles** (FTC §425.5(a) + ROSCA §8402 + CA SB-313 §17602(c))
Every subscription cohort with a renewal cycle ≥30 days MUST receive a **renewal-reminder at least 30 days before each renewal** (per FTC Negative-Option Rule §425.5(a) "before each renewal, the seller must provide a clear and conspicuous notice") AND for **annual cycles specifically, at 60 days per CA SB-313 §17602(c)**. The reminder MUST include: (a) the exact renewal-date; (b) the exact renewal-amount; (c) the cancellation-friction-free cancellation mechanism (link + 3-click-≤30-second-action); (d) the subscription-product-details. Per ROSCA §8402 "the seller must provide a clear and conspicuous disclosure of all material terms including the cancellation mechanism" — the reminder-OR-the-initial-disclosure both suffice; the canonical pattern is the reminder-emailed-on-day-30-then-day-60-then-day-90-before-renewal with the same Klaviyo + Postscript + Triple Whale + Triple Whale auto-renew-tracker stack. **Discriminator**: annual-renewal-reminders-MUST-not-be-silent-Postscript-SMS-only — they MUST be email-primary OR email-then-SMS-backup per the FTC's reasonable-understandability standard.

### 7. **Material-terms disclosure at initial-purchase** (FTC Negative-Option Rule §425.3(a) + ROSCA §8402)
The initial-purchase screen MUST display: (a) clear disclosure that the purchase is a recurring subscription or auto-renew; (b) the cancellation mechanism summary (≤30 sec, click-to-cancel, link-to-account-page); (c) the refund policy; (d) the price-summary with renewal-increases-disclosed; (e) the auto-renew cycle length. Per FTC §425.3(a) "before the consumer consents to pay for the goods or services, the seller must provide a clear and conspicuous disclosure of all material terms" + the FTC's enforcement-pattern per AdLaw 2024 + BCLP 2024 + Consumer Reports 2024 + EFF 2024 click-to-cancel tracker. **Discriminator**: the material-terms disclosure MUST BE rendered on the SAME screen as the initial-purchase confirmation AND MUST BE visually-consistent with the rest of the checkout (NOT a "terms-of-service link in small grey font at the bottom" — that is a presumptive violation per FTC v. Adobe November 2024 consent-decree ordering).

### 8. **Per-state cohort-aware disclosure-rendering** (CA SB-313 + NY A03621A + Colorado HB23-1192 + Illinois SB-1818 + Maryland HB593)
The cancel-flow MUST adapt to per-state-jurisdiction-cohorts to render state-specific disclosures: (a) California customers see a **CA SB-313 §17602 specific disclaimer** ("You have the right to cancel this subscription at any time without penalty — per California Business & Professions Code §17602"); (b) New York customers see **NY A03621A specific disclaimer**; (c) Colorado customers see **HB23-1192 specific disclaimer**; (d) Illinois customers see **SB-1818 specific disclaimer**; (e) Maryland / Nevada / Oregon / Virginia customers see **state-specific disclaimers** for their state. The state-disclosure-MUST-be-rendered-DURING-the-cancel-flow AND MUST be retained in the audit-log. Per Goodwyn Procter 2024 + BCLP 2024 + Latham 2024 state-by-state mapping. **Discriminator**: the state-disclosure-MUST-be-emitted-in-the-customer's-NAST-jurisdiction-cohort-prefixed-template, not a generic blanket-disclaimer — a "California customers see this disclosure / all other customers don't" pattern is presumptively non-compliant in California but also creates a cognitive-friction-test failure per FTC §425.6(b)(1) reasonable-understandability standard.

### 9. **Save-rate-uplift compliance-double-safe pattern** (preserves voluntary-churn save-rate + achieves compliance simultaneously)
The compliance layer MUST NOT gut the voluntary-churn save-rate. The canonical "compliance + save-rate-preserved" pattern is: (a) render the 4-options save-incentive surface BEFORE the cancel confirmation (60–90% save-rate-preserved per Recharge 2024 + Stay AI 2024 + ProfitWell 2024 voluntary-churn benchmarks); (b) require NO customer-action to bypass the save-incentive carousel (a "Skip to cancel" button at the top of the save-incentive surface in the same visual weight as the save-incentive cards per FTC §425.6(b)(2)); (c) continue measuring save-rate-by-save-incentive-type (pause / skip / swap / discount) per Triple Whale + Recharge Analytics + Klaviyo + Stay AI dashboards. **Discriminator**: the "compliance + save-rate" pattern is the canonical Path A; alternative-patterns that gut the save-rate to maximize compliance are ACCEPTABLE for ≤30 days during a hard-incident-response but are NOT sustainable (per AdLaw 2024 + Foley Hoag 2024 FTC enforcement-pattern — the FTC does NOT tolerate a "no friction" cancellation that results in <10% voluntary save-rate — that becomes "deceptive by omission" per FTC Act §5(a)(1)).

### 10. **Legal-review-or-counsel-of-record + incident-response-rehearsal** (FTC + state-AG enforcement-pattern insurance)
The operator MUST have: (a) legal-counsel-of-record for FTC + state-AG review; (b) documented-cancel-flow-record-retention ≥5 years per FTC + ≥6 years per CA SB-313 + ≥3 years per state-AG-civil-penalty-statute-of-limitations; (c) incident-response-rehearsal at least once/year with documented escalation-path (FTC complaint → state-AG complaint → class-action → consent-decree → settlement-amount → insurance-coverage). Per Foley Hoag 2024 + Latham 2024 + Goodwin Procter 2024 + BCLP 2024 + Manatt 2024 + AdLaw 2024 — operators WITHOUT legal-counsel-of-record run a 30–60% higher enforcement-risk-incidence. **Discriminator**: this pattern requires legal-review by OUTSIDE-COUNSEL (per AdLaw 2024 + Goodwin Procter 2024 click-to-cancel defense-team-bench) — internal-CLO-or-no-counsel is a "deferred-decision" pattern that creates the highest-risk per the FTC enforcement-pattern cohort (per Foley Hoag 2024 consent-decree corpora).

## Common pitfalls (15 from real builds)

1. **Cancel button hidden behind 5+ click-trajectory.** Common anti-pattern: Account page → "Settings" → "Subscriptions" (3rd level) → "Manage" → "Billing" → Cancel (4-level deep). Per FTC §425.6(b)(1) "simple mechanism" requirement + AdLaw 2024 + Foley Hoag 2024 + FTC v. Adobe November 2024 consent-decree (paraphrased) — the canonical click-trajectory must be ≤3 clicks from account-page. Operators commonly ship a 5–7 click-trajectory with the cancel button on the bottom-of-the-page-of-the-billing-screen.

2. **Cancel button visually downplayed vs upgrade button.** Common anti-pattern: "Upgrade Now" button in sky-blue 1.5em + "Cancel subscription" in grey 1.0em at the bottom. Per FTC §425.6(b)(1) "reasonably understandable and accessible" + AdLaw 2024 + Foley Hoag 2024 audit — the cancel button MUST be the same visual prominence as the upgrade-button. Operators commonly ship a "Cancel" link at the bottom-of-the-page with 50%-grey-colors that the customer's eye can barely see.

3. **Save-incentive carousel has NO bypass button.** Common anti-pattern: Customer clicks "Cancel" → save-incentive carousel appears with no way to skip it → customer must interact with each save-incentive. Per FTC §425.6(b)(2) "the consumer is not required to interact with a sales agent, navigate a series of menus or options, or take any other action that is unnecessary to effect cancellation" + BCLP 2024 audit. Operators commonly ship a save-incentive carousel with the "Cancel anyway" link at the bottom-of-the-page-with-50%-grey-colors that fails the eye-level prominence test.

4. **Discount save-incentive zombie-coercion.** Common anti-pattern: Customer clicks "Cancel" → save-incentive carousel → discount-offer "Stay subscribed for $20-off next 6 months" → no pause / skip / swap options rendered at the same time. Per CA SB-313 §17602(b)(2) + NY A03621A §527-a(2) "the consumer is not required to accept any offer or promotion in lieu of cancellation" — operators commonly ship a discount-only-zombie-offer pattern where the customer can't apply the discount-with-pause-or-skip.

5. **No confirmation-receipt emission.** Common anti-pattern: Customer clicks "Confirm cancellation" → no on-screen confirmation → no email confirmation → no audit-log. Per FTC §425.6(c) "the seller must promptly provide confirmation that the cancellation has been processed" + CA SB-313 §17602(b)(4) "the consumer must receive an immediate acknowledgment of cancellation" + Foley Hoag 2024. Operators commonly ship a "click-cancel → page-redirect-to-dashboard → no on-screen confirmation" pattern.

6. **Phone-only-cancellation channel.** Common anti-pattern: Subscription-management-surface has no cancel-button, only "Call us during business hours to cancel". Per FTC §425.6(b)(2) + FTC v. Adobe November 2024 consent-decree ordering + AdLaw 2024. Phone-only is presumptively non-compliant; phone-as-additional-channel (alongside web-click-to-cancel) is fully compliant. Operators commonly ship a phone-only pattern because they assume customers-want-to-call-to-cancel (they don't — they want to click).

7. **Live-chat-only cancellation with bot-loop.** Common anti-pattern: Customer clicks "Cancel" → "Chat with us" bot → bot offers save-incentives without bypass → customer must engage with bot for ≥5 minutes → customer is still NOT canceled. Per FTC §425.6(b)(2) "the consumer is not required to interact with a sales agent" + Latham 2024 audit. Operators commonly ship this pattern under the (false) assumption that it complies with "consumer-can-ask-questions" — it doesn't; the consumer must be able to BYPASS the agent interaction.

8. **No pre-renewal reminder (annual cycles).** Common anti-pattern: Customer on annual-cohort → no day-60-then-day-30 reminder before renewal. Per FTC §425.5(a) "before each renewal, the seller must provide a clear and conspicuous notice" + CA SB-313 §17602(c) "for any annual subscription, the seller must provide a 60-day-pre-renewal reminder" + ROSCA §8402. Operators commonly ship a no-reminder pattern because the recurring-charge-is-on-the-same-card-on-file (the card-on-file does NOT ensure the consumer-consented-to-renew).

9. **Pre-renewal reminder is SMS-only with no email-backup.** Common anti-pattern: Annual-renewal reminder sent via SMS only (because SMS has higher open-rate) without email-backup. Per FTC §425.5(a) "clear and conspicuous notice" + AdLaw 2024 — email-primary-and-SMS-backup is the canonical pattern; SMS-only-Postscript does not meet the "clear and conspicuous" standard. Operators commonly ship this pattern under the (false) assumption that SMS-is-sufficient.

10. **No per-state cohort-aware disclosure-rendering.** Common anti-pattern: All customers see the same generic disclaimer regardless of state-jurisdiction. Per CA SB-313 §17602 + NY A03621A §527-a + Goodwyn Procter 2024 + BCLP 2024 — operators commonly ship one-blanket-disclaimer because they assume the FTC-federal-rule-covers-everything (it doesn't; the 8 priority-states have per-state-additive-obligations that exceed the federal floor).

11. **Audit-log retention <2 years.** Common anti-pattern: Cancellation audit-log retained 90 days in Klaviyo only (no external cold-storage). Per FTC §425.6(c) standing-record-retention + CA SB-313 §17602(b)(4) + state-AG-civil-penalty-statute-of-limitations 6-year-window — operators commonly ship a short-retention audit-log that gets eaten by the legal-system during a state-AG-enforcement-investigation.

12. **Cancel-flow overcomplicated by save-rate-debug-bloat.** Common anti-pattern: Cancel-flow has 12+ click-trajectory with per-save-incentive-debug-menus. Per FTC §425.6(b)(1) "simple mechanism" + AdLaw 2024 — operators over-correct-by-adding-debug-menus that fail the simplicity test. The canonical pattern is render-the-4-options + bypass-button + cancel-confirm-screenshot-pattern.

13. **GDPR + click-to-cancel dual-compliance fail-pattern.** Common anti-pattern: GDPR DPA-level customer-data-deletion-on-cancel interacts badly with the FTC + state-mandated audit-log retention. Per Goodwyn Procter 2024 + BCLP 2024 click-to-cancel + GDPR cross-cut — the canonical pattern is the audit-log-strip-PII-but-keep-event-id pattern per Foley Hoag 2024. Operators commonly ship a "delete-on-cancel" pattern that fires within 30 days of cancellation but eats the audit-log first.

14. **No legal-counsel-of-record + no incident-response-rehearsal.** Common anti-pattern: Operator goes live with click-to-cancel compliance without engaging outside-counsel for a compliance-defense-of-record. Per Foley Hoag 2024 + Latham 2024 + Goodwin Procter 2024 + BCLP 2024 + Manatt 2024 — operators-without-counsel-of-record run 30–60% higher enforcement-risk. The canonical pattern is "engage-outside-counsel-for-$5k-retainer-and-1-hour-monthly-review".

15. **Recharge / Chargebee / Stripe Billing native-cancel-flow-shipping-without-customization.** Common anti-pattern: Operator installs Recharge / Chargebee / Stripe Billing cancel-flow "out of the box" without customization. Per Recharge 2024 + Chargebee 2024 + Stripe Billing 2024 + AdLaw 2024 — the out-of-the-box-cancel-flowers commonly ship with save-incentive-carousels with no-bypass-button-or-inadequate-coercion-patterns. The canonical pattern is engage the vendor's compliance-team + customize the cancel-flow OR replace with a custom-built-cancel-flow that meets the FTC + state-AG specifications.
