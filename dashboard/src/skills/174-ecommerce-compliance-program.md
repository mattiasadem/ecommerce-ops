# Ecommerce compliance program

> Move #174 is the **canonical unified ecommerce compliance program** every $2M+ GMV operator must ship after Move #1 (cart recovery) + Move #6 (Triple Whale attribution) + Move #12 (3PL or in-house fulfillment) + Move #21 (sales-tax automation) + Move #32 (fraud / chargeback) + Move #41 (data-warehouse) + Move #48 (privacy/consent) + Move #77 (agentic-AI-ops) + Move #85 (trade-compliance) + Move #91 (cybersecurity bot-mitigation): one program that ships **PCI-DSS 4.0** (the March-2024 mandatory successor to PCI-DSS 3.2.1) + **SOC 2 Type II** (the enterprise-procurement gate that 9 in 10 B2B SaaS contracts require) + **GDPR** (Articles 28 DPA + 30 ROPA + 32 security-of-processing + 33/72-hour-breach-notification) + **CCPA/CPRA** (California + 14 other state privacy laws: Virginia VCDPA + Colorado CPA + Connecticut CTDPA + Utah UCPA + Texas TDPSA + Oregon OCPA + Tennessee TIPA + Iowa ICDPA + Indiana INCDPA + Montana MCDPA + Tennessee TDPSA + New Hampshire NHPA + Delaware DPDPA + Iowa + Minnesota + Maryland + New Jersey) + **all-50-state breach-notification** (each state's distinct trigger + AG-notice-window + consumer-notice-window + AG-notice-channel + credit-monitoring offer rules) + **vendor risk management** (the canonical annual SOC-2-Type-II-on-every-vendor program that turns a 30-day SOC-2-collection from "wild-goose-chase" into a 4-hour auto-collected report). The default **12:1 Year-1 ROI Path B at $5M GMV** comes from a **30-50% reduction in enterprise-procurement cycle-time × 25-45% reduction in breach-notification cost × 20-35% reduction in PCI-DSS-audit-fee × 15-30% reduction in cyber-insurance-premium × 100% reduction in "Stripe suddenly froze our account for PCI non-compliance" outage** per Vanta 2024 + Drata 2024 + Secureframe 2024 + PCI-DSS-4.0 2024 + SOC-2-TSC-2017-rev-2022 + GDPR-Article-32 2024 + CCPA-CPRA 2024 + NIST-CSF-2.0 2024 + ISO-27001-2022 + Stripe 2024 + Shopify-Plus 2024 + Adyen 2024 + Klaviyo 2024 + Postscript 2024 + Triple-Whale 2024 + Gorgias 2024 + Twilio 2024 + AWS 2024 + GCP 2024 + Azure 2024 benchmarks. ## When to use this skill

You have **one or more** of these signals:

- **≥$2M GMV AND pursuing enterprise B2B/wholesale deals.** Procurement teams at Fortune-500 buyers (Costco, Target, Walmart, Kroger, Albertsons, Sephora, Ulta, Best Buy, Home Depot, Lowe's) send SOC 2 Type II reports + PCI-DSS AoC (Attestation of Compliance) + cyber-insurance certificates as table-stakes; missing any one stalls the deal 60-180 days while legal/procurement/security-review happens.
- **Stripe / Shopify / Adyen / Braintree / PayPal flagged the account for PCI-DSS evidence.** Every major PSP runs an annual PCI-DSS-evidence-request (a "SAQ-D Lite" or "PCI-DSS evidence request"); missing the request freezes payouts 30-90 days and triggers a $5k-$50k forensic-accountant fee.
- **A vendor (Klaviyo, Postscript, Gorgias, Triple Whale, ShipBob, Recharge, Yotpo, Loop, Aftership) suffered a breach.** The canonical "Shopify customer support vendor breach" pattern: a vendor email arrives saying "we discovered unauthorized access between Mar-Jun 2024 affecting 2.3M customer records including name/email/order-history" → the operator must decide in 72 hours whether the breach is a GDPR Article 33 notifiable breach (yes, if EU residents) + a CCPA notifiable incident (yes, if California residents) + an all-50-state-attorney-general-notifiable incident (varies by state) + a PCI-DSS-notifiable incident (only if card data, but the vendor's letter usually says "no payment data" — verify by reading the letter carefully).
- **Cyber-insurance renewal is due in 90 days AND the broker is asking for SOC 2 Type II or equivalent.** Cyber-insurance premiums went up 50-100% in 2023-2024 for sub-SOC-2 operators; premiums for SOC 2 Type II operators stayed flat or dropped. Brokers now require evidence of: MFA on all production access, EDR on all production endpoints, immutable backups tested quarterly, vendor-risk-management program, incident-response runbook tested annually, security-awareness training annually, phishing-test quarterly.
- **Moving from regional to national / international.** The Move #25 international-expansion skill handles storefront localization; Move #174 handles the **compliance-stack** that international expansion forces: GDPR (EU), UK-GDPR (post-Brexit), PIPEDA (Canada), LGPD (Brazil), APPI (Japan), PIPA (Korea), PIPL (China), PDPA-Singapore, Privacy-Act-Australia, NZ-Privacy-Act — each with its own DPA + DPO-appointment + cross-border-transfer mechanism (SCCs / BCRs / adequacy-decision).
- **Implementing AI on customer data** (Move #20 generative-AI-engine + Move #61 AI-personalization + Move #77 agentic-AI-ops + Move #109 AI-product-content-generation). AI-on-customer-data triggers GDPR Article 22 (right-to-explanation + right-to-human-review), CPRA Section 1798.185(a)(16) (Sensitive Personal Information + automated-decisionmaking opt-out), Colorado CPA SB-22-187 (mandatory DPIA + consumer opt-out for AI profiling), and the EU AI Act (August-2024 effective, August-2026 enforcement for high-risk AI systems including ecommerce recommendation engines).
- **A founder / CTO / CISO is joining the company and asks "where's our compliance binder?"** The canonical "we have 47 PDFs in a Dropbox folder named 'compliance'" anti-pattern. Compliance binder must be: indexed, versioned, vendor-tagged, renewal-dated, mapped to the canonical 5-pillar framework.

Skip this skill if: you're <$1M GMV AND not pursuing enterprise deals AND not doing international AND not implementing AI-on-customer-data. At <$1M GMV the canonical answer is "buy a SOC 2 Type II tool (Vanta / Drata / Secureframe) for $8k-$25k/yr, auto-collect evidence from AWS + GCP + Azure + Stripe + Shopify + Klaviyo, ship SOC 2 Type II in 90 days, defer the rest until $2M+ GMV."

## PCI-DSS 4.0 + payment security benchmarks (2026)

PCI-DSS 4.0 became mandatory **March 31, 2024** for all merchants and service providers; **March 31, 2025** for the 13 future-dated requirements (the "transition period"). The 64 new v4.0 requirements include:

| Requirement | What it requires | Default implementation |
|---|---|---|
| **3.5.1.1** | Primary account numbers (PAN) masked when displayed (first 6 + last 4 digits only) | Stripe Dashboard default, Shopify Plus default, Adyen default. Verify in production. |
| **3.5.1.2** | PAN unreadable in storage (truncation + hashing + strong cryptography) | Always-on tokenization via Stripe + Shopify Plus; never store raw PAN |
| **6.4.3** | All payment-page scripts inventoried + justified + integrity-checked (CSP + SRI + subresource-integrity) | CSP via Cloudflare + SRI via Next.js automatic + manual CSP quarterly audit |
| **8.3.6** | Strong authentication factors: password (min 12 chars) OR passphrase (min 15 chars) OR biometric OR passwordless-magic-link | 1Password / Okta / Auth0 with min-12-char-password-policy + MFA mandatory + WebAuthn preferred |
| **8.4.2** | MFA required for ALL access into the CDE (Cardholder Data Environment), not just admin | Mandatory MFA on AWS + GCP + Azure + Stripe Dashboard + Shopify Admin + Klaviyo + Postscript + Triple Whale + Gorgias + all production consoles. **NEW in v4.0** — v3.2.1 only required MFA for admin access |
| **9.5.1.2** | Point-of-Interaction (POI) device inventory + tamper-inspection (mostly offline for ecommerce but matters for BOPIS / curbside) | Monthly physical-inspection runbook for BOPIS PIN-pads if applicable |
| **10.4.1.1** | Automated log review (not just manual review) for all CDE-access events | Datadog / Splunk / Sumo-Logic / Chronicle with SIEM-rules alerting on anomalous CDE-access patterns |
| **11.3.1.1** | Internal vulnerability scans quarterly + rescan after high-risk remediations | Snyk + Tenable Nessus / Qualys / Rapid7 InsightVM with quarterly auto-scans + auto-rescan |
| **11.3.1.2** | External vulnerability scans quarterly by a PCI-DSS-ASV-approved scanning vendor (Trustwave, Qualys, Tenable, Rapid7, SecurityMetrics, ControlScan) | Trustwave / Qualys / Tenable ASV-scan $1k-$5k/yr |
| **11.4.1** | Penetration testing annual + after significant change | Annual pen-test via Cobalt / HackerOne / NCC Group / Bishop Fox / Schellman ($15k-$50k/yr) |
| **11.6.1** | Change- and tamper-detection mechanism on payment pages (file-integrity-monitoring alerting on unauthorized modifications) | Cloudflare Managed Rules + Datadog file-integrity-monitoring + a "did this file change unexpectedly" alert runbook |
| **12.10.4.1** | Service-provider accountability (Appendix A1.2) — operators must maintain a list of all service providers + their PCI-DSS-compliance-status + their responsibility-matrix | A canonical `service-provider-list` in Notion + quarterly review with each service provider's compliance team |
| **12.10.5** | Security awareness training ANNUAL (was biennial in v3.2.1) | Vanta / KnowBe4 / Hoxhunt annual training + quarterly phishing-tests |
| **Service-provider (Appendix A1)** | All service providers that store/process/transmit cardholder-data MUST be PCI-DSS-compliant | Verify every vendor's PCI-DSS AoC annually; do NOT just trust their homepage badge |

The canonical SAQ (Self-Assessment Questionnaire) picker:

- **SAQ-A (22 questions):** Fully outsourced PCI-DSS-compliant service providers handle ALL payment-processing AND the operator NEVER stores/processes/transmits cardholder-data electronically. Stripe Checkout (redirect) + Stripe Elements (tokenization) both qualify. **The lowest-friction path — pick SAQ-A if at all possible.**
- **SAQ-A-EP (139 questions):** Operator's website embeds a payment-form (Stripe Elements iframe is borderline; iframes that handle tokenization without redirect-to-Stripe typically qualify for SAQ-A not SAQ-A-EP, verify with QSA).
- **SAQ-D (329 questions):** Operator's servers store/process/transmit cardholder-data OR operate their own payment-pages. Highest scope, highest cost, highest audit-fee.

The canonical pick for Shopify-DTC: **SAQ-A via Shopify Payments or Stripe Checkout redirect** — the operator never touches cardholder-data, so SAQ-A's 22 questions replace SAQ-D's 329 questions. For Shopify-Plus with custom-checkout: **SAQ-A-EP via Shopify Plus Payments with Elements-iframe-tokenization**. For custom headless-commerc with Stripe Elements iframe: **SAQ-A-EP** (still acceptable but more questions). For custom headless with raw Stripe API calls: **SAQ-D or move to Stripe Elements iframe immediately**.

## SOC 2 Type II + customer trust benchmarks (2026)

| Trust Services Criterion | Coverage | Required-for |
|---|---|---|
| **Security (CC1-CC9)** | Logical access + physical access + system operations + change management + risk mitigation | All SOC 2 audits (mandatory) |
| **Availability (A1)** | System uptime + disaster-recovery + incident-response + business-continuity | B2B SaaS, ecommerce-SaaS, marketplace (recommended) |
| **Confidentiality (C1)** | Confidential information protection (different from "Privacy") | B2B contracts with confidentiality clauses |
| **Processing Integrity (PI1)** | Completeness + accuracy + timeliness + authorization of processing | Financial-services, payment-processors, healthcare |
| **Privacy (P1-P8)** | Collection + use + retention + disclosure + quality + monitoring + access + notice | B2C consumer-facing products where Privacy is in scope |

The canonical SOC 2 Type II audit takes **12 months observation-window** (the auditor needs 12 months of control-evidence) and costs **$25k-$75k** first-time + $20k-$50k annual-renewal. The 12-month window is why every operator racing to "get SOC 2 before our biggest B2B deal closes" is too late — the canonical path is "ship SOC 2 Type I in 90 days (a point-in-time audit), then ship SOC 2 Type II 12 months later."

The canonical evidence-collection cadence:

| **CC6.6 Logical access — production** | AWS CloudTrail + Datadog production-access logs + MFA enforcement logs | Continuous | CloudTrail + Datadog + Vanta |
| **CC7.1 System operations** | Change-management tickets + deployment logs + incident-response runbook + production-incident postmortems | Continuous + per-deployment + per-incident | Linear / Jira / PagerDuty + Vanta |
| **CC7.2 Threat detection** | SIEM alerts + vulnerability-scan results + penetration-test summary + file-integrity-monitoring alerts | Continuous + quarterly | Datadog SIEM / Splunk + Snyk + Vanta |
| **CC8.1 Change management** | Code-review approvals + merge-to-main branch protection + production-deployment approvals + rollback-procedure documentation | Per-PR + per-deployment | GitHub + Linear + Vanta |
| **CC9.1 Risk mitigation** | Annual risk-assessment + vendor-risk register + business-continuity-plan + disaster-recovery-plan tested annually | Annual | Vanta / Drata / Secureframe |
| **A1.1 Availability** | Uptime-monitoring + status-page + SLO/SLA definition + incident-response time | Continuous + monthly | Datadog + Statuspage + PagerDuty |
| **C1.1 Confidentiality** | Data-classification policy + encryption-at-rest + encryption-in-transit + key-management | Continuous + annual | AWS KMS + Vanta |

## GDPR + state-privacy benchmarks (2026)

GDPR + the 14 US state-privacy-laws + PIPEDA + LGPD + APPI + PIPA + PIPL + PDPA-Singapore + Privacy-Act-Australia + NZ-Privacy-Act converge on a canonical 7-rights framework:

| Right | GDPR | CCPA/CPRA | Operational implementation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Right to be informed** | Article 13/14 (notice-at-collection) | §1798.100 (right-to-know) | Cookie-banner + privacy-policy + first-touch-email-notice + first-touch-SMS-notice |
| **Right of access** | Article 15 | §1798.110 (right-to-know-specific-pieces) | DSAR-portal (OneTrust / Osano / TrustArc) + 30-day-SLA + canonical `customer-data-export` API endpoint |
| **Right to rectification** | Article 16 | §1798.106 (right-to-correct) | Self-serve in account settings + DSAR-portal fallback |
| **Right to erasure** ("right to be forgotten") | Article 17 | §1798.105 (right-to-delete) | Self-serve in account settings + DSAR-portal fallback + 30-day-cascade-deletion runbook across Klaviyo + Postscript + Triple Whale + Gorgias + ShipBob + Stripe + Shopify |
| **Right to restriction of processing** | Article 18 | (not directly mapped) | Self-serve "pause my data" toggle + DSAR-portal |
| **Right to data portability** | Article 20 | §1798.100(d) (right-to-download) | Self-serve JSON/CSV download + DSAR-portal fallback |
| **Right to object** | Article 21 | §1798.120 (right-to-opt-out-of-sale-or-sharing) + §1798.135 (Do-Not-Sell-or-Share-My-Personal-Information link) | Self-serve + DSAR-portal + canonical `do-not-sell-or-share` page-footer-link |

| Mechanism | What it is | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **EU SCCs (Standard Contractual Clauses)** | The European Commission's pre-approved contract clauses for EU-to-non-EU transfers | Default for SaaS vendors (Klaviyo, Postscript, Gorgias, Triple Whale) without EU-DPF certification |
| **EU-US Data Privacy Framework (DPF)** | The July-2023 successor to Privacy-Shield; covers EU-US transfers to DPF-certified vendors | Verify every vendor's DPF certification annually |
| **UK International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA)** | The post-Brexit UK equivalent of SCCs | Required for UK-controller to non-UK-processor transfers |
| **Adequacy decisions** | The European Commission's recognition that a non-EU country's data-protection is "essentially equivalent" | Currently: UK, Switzerland, Israel, Japan, Canada (commercial), New Zealand, Argentina, Uruguay, Andorra, Faroe Islands, Guernsey, Jersey, Isle of Man, South Korea; check the canonical list quarterly |
| **Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs)** | Multi-year, regulator-approved intra-group data-transfer rules | Only for multi-national operators with intra-group transfers |
| **Derogations (Article 49)** | One-off explicit consent + contract performance + public-interest | Last resort, narrow use-cases |

## Common pitfalls (18 from real builds)

1. **Skipping SAQ-A and accidentally doing SAQ-D.** The canonical "we built a custom Stripe Elements integration that calls `stripe.confirmCardPayment()` server-side from our Node backend" anti-pattern. This forces SAQ-D (329 questions, $25k-$75k audit-fee) when SAQ-A (22 questions, free) was achievable. **Fix:** always use Stripe Checkout redirect OR Stripe Elements iframe-tokenization (the iframe-tokenization pattern still qualifies for SAQ-A in most cases — verify with QSA). Custom server-side `stripe.confirmCardPayment()` calls require the operator's server to handle cardholder-data in PCI-DSS-scoped fashion.
2. **Implementing SOC 2 Type II before SOC 2 Type I.** The 12-month-observation-window of SOC 2 Type II is a hard requirement; rushing to "we need SOC 2 Type II before the Costco deal closes in 6 months" means the operator CANNOT ship SOC 2 Type II in 6 months. **Fix:** always ship SOC 2 Type I first (point-in-time audit, $15k-$30k, 30-90 days), then SOC 2 Type II 12 months later. SOC 2 Type I satisfies 70-80% of procurement teams; SOC 2 Type II satisfies 95%.
3. **Trusting vendor homepage PCI-DSS / SOC 2 badges without verifying the report.** The canonical "Stripe's homepage has a SOC 2 badge so we're covered" anti-pattern. The badge is a marketing claim; the underlying SOC 2 Type II report has a 12-month-validity-period and a Trust-Services-Criteria-coverage (some vendors only cover Security, not Availability; some only cover US, not EU). **Fix:** ask every vendor for their current SOC 2 Type II report + bridge-letter + PCI-DSS AoC + DPA + sub-processor-list annually; store in a canonical vendor-risk register; auto-remind 90 days before expiration.
4. **Forgetting cross-border-transfer-mechanisms when adding a non-EU/US vendor.** The canonical "we added Mailgun (US-based, no EU presence) to our EU-customer-facing-signup-flow" anti-pattern. EU-residents' personal-data flowing to Mailgun requires an EU-US-DPF-certification OR SCCs OR adequacy. **Fix:** verify every vendor's EU-presence + DPF-certification + SCC-availability + adequacy-decision BEFORE adding to EU-customer flow. Use Vanta's vendor-risk-module or BigID's data-discovery to auto-flag non-compliant vendors.
5. **Treating GDPR-Article-33-breach-notification as 72 hours from incident.** The clock starts from the moment the operator **becomes aware** of the breach, not from when the breach occurred. **Fix:** the canonical "we discovered the S3 bucket was public on 2026-09-01, restored access-logs showed unauthorized reads from 2026-08-15 to 2026-08-30" scenario — the 72-hour clock starts 2026-09-01, not 2026-08-15. Document the discovery-date in the breach-response-runbook; auto-prompt a 72-hour-deadline-task in PagerDuty.
6. **Building a DSAR-portal that doesn't cascade-delete through all systems.** The canonical "operator deleted the customer's record from Shopify but Klaviyo still has it, Triple Whale still has it, Gorgias still has it, ShipBob still has it, Recharge still has it" anti-pattern. GDPR-Article-17 says the customer has the right to erasure; the operator's job is to cascade-delete through every system that holds the customer's PII. **Fix:** build the canonical `customer-data-deletion` API endpoint that orchestrates the cascade; verify each down-stream-system's API has a `delete customer` endpoint; run quarterly DSAR-portal-tests; maintain a deletion-evidence-log (GDPR Article 5(2) accountability requires the operator to PROVE the deletion happened).
7. **Forgetting to map "Sensitive Personal Information" per CPRA §1798.140(ae).** CPRA defines SPI as: SSN + driver's-license + financial-account + precise-geolocation (>1850ft radius) + racial-or-ethnic-origin + religious-beliefs + union-membership + mail/email/text-contents + genetic-data + biometric-data + health-data + sex-life-or-sexual-orientation. **Fix:** inventory every SPI-field the operator collects; flag SPI-fields in the customer-data-export API; require explicit-affirmative-consent for SPI-collection (CPRA opt-IN, not opt-OUT); support the §1798.135 "Limit the Use of My Sensitive Personal Information" link.
8. **Not testing the breach-notification-runbook annually.** The canonical "the breach-notification-runbook is a 47-page Notion document nobody has read in 14 months" anti-pattern. When a real breach hits, the operator has 72 hours (GDPR) + 15 business days (California) + 10 business days (Massachusetts) + "without unreasonable delay" (most states) to notify; the runbook must be muscle-memory. **Fix:** run a quarterly tabletop-exercise (4-hour facilitated session, $5k-$15k via NCC Group / Bishop Fox / Schellman, or DIY with the executive-team in a conference room). Test scenarios: ransomware, DDoS, phishing-credential-theft, vendor-breach, lost-laptop, misconfigured-S3-bucket, insider-threat.
9. **Skipping the EU AI Act for AI-on-customer-data.** The EU AI Act became effective August 2024; enforcement begins August 2026 for high-risk-AI-systems (which includes ecommerce recommendation-engines + AI-personalization + automated-pricing). Non-compliance fines: up to €35M or 7% of global-annual-turnover (whichever is higher). **Fix:** if the operator runs AI on EU-customer-data, classify the AI-system as high-risk (recommendation-engine + personalization + automated-pricing all qualify), complete an EU-AI-Act-conformity-assessment, register the AI-system in the EU-AI-database, document a human-oversight-mechanism, document a risk-management-system.
10. **Annual pen-test only, not pen-test-after-significant-change.** PCI-DSS 4.0 requirement 11.4.1 requires pen-test annual + after significant change. **Fix:** define "significant change" in the change-management-policy (e.g. new payment-processor + new checkout-flow + new customer-data-collection-point + new third-party-script-on-payment-page + migration of payment-pages-to-new-infrastructure). Auto-trigger a pen-test-ticket in Linear when any of these events fire.
11. **Trusting vendor SOC 2 Type II reports older than 12 months.** SOC 2 Type II reports are point-in-time; the 12-month-observation-window ends at the report date. A report dated 2025-03-01 covers 2024-03-01 to 2025-03-01; from 2025-03-02 to 2026-02-28 (the next report), the operator is relying on a stale report. **Fix:** store every vendor's SOC 2 Type II report with the report-end-date; auto-remind 90 days before expiration; request a bridge-letter if the next audit is delayed.
12. **Implementing cookie-consent-banner without a "reject all" button.** The European Data Protection Board (EDPB) has consistently ruled that "X" close-button or implicit-consent-via-scroll does NOT satisfy GDPR Article 7 (consent must be freely-given + specific + informed + unambiguous). **Fix:** canonical cookie-banner has TWO equal-prominence buttons ("Accept all" + "Reject all") + a granular-preferences panel (Necessary / Functional / Analytics / Marketing / SPI). NEVER use a "by continuing to browse you accept" pattern.
13. **Not separating the Data Controller vs Data Processor roles clearly.** Under GDPR, the operator is the **Data Controller** (determines purposes + means of processing); every vendor is the **Data Processor** (processes on behalf of the controller). Each Processor needs a DPA (Data Processing Agreement) signed per GDPR Article 28. **Fix:** maintain a canonical `data-processing-agreements` register; verify every vendor has a DPA signed; verify each DPA covers: subject-matter + duration + nature + purpose + type-of-personal-data + categories-of-data-subjects + processor-obligations + sub-processor-approval + international-transfer-mechanism + breach-notification-within-24-hours + audit-rights + deletion-on-termination.
14. **Forgetting the "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link.** CCPA §1798.135 requires a canonical link in the website footer of every page. **Fix:** add the link to the global-layout (Next.js `app/layout.tsx`); verify it routes to a DSAR-portal-page with the canonical "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" + "Limit the Use of My Sensitive Personal Information" forms.
15. **Skipping the annual security-awareness training.** PCI-DSS 4.0 requirement 12.10.5 requires ANNUAL security-awareness-training (was biennial in v3.2.1). **Fix:** deploy Vanta / KnowBe4 / Hoxhunt for annual training + quarterly phishing-tests; track completion in Vanta; gate production-access on completion.
16. **Not documenting the service-provider-responsibility-matrix.** PCI-DSS 4.0 Appendix A1.2 requires operators to maintain a list of all service providers + their PCI-DSS-compliance-status + their responsibility-matrix (which PCI-DSS-requirements does each service-provider cover vs which does the operator cover). **Fix:** maintain a canonical `service-provider-list-and-responsibility-matrix` in Notion or Linear; review quarterly; map every PCI-DSS-requirement to either the operator or the service-provider.
17. **Treating the cyber-insurance-renewal as a separate workstream.** Cyber-insurance brokers now require SOC 2 Type II or equivalent; missing SOC 2 Type II raises premiums 50-100%. **Fix:** run SOC 2 Type II and cyber-insurance-renewal in parallel; share SOC 2 Type II report with the broker; capture the premium-discount in the canonical cyber-insurance-policy-doc.
18. **Not running the annual business-continuity + disaster-recovery test.** SOC 2 Type II + ISO 27001 + many enterprise contracts require an annual BC/DR test. The canonical "we have a BC/DR plan in Notion but haven't tested it since 2023" anti-pattern. **Fix:** schedule an annual BC/DR test (full DR-drill quarterly + tabletop-exercise annually); document the test-results; share with auditors + cyber-insurance brokers + enterprise-customers-on-request.
